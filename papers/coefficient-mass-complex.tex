\begin{abstract}
Let $b_k(F)$ be the $k$-th largest nonleading coefficient magnitude of
$F=\sum_{j\le D}f_jx^j$ and $\Lambda(F)=\sum_j\log^+|f_j|$.  At real roots
$r_i\ge2$ a companion paper gives $b_k(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_{i\ge k}(r_i-1)$ for
every multiple and charges the $i$-th smallest root $i$ times in $\Lambda$.
Purely imaginary pairs $\pm ic$ reduce by multisection exactly to real roots
$c^2$: every $b_k$ and $\Lambda$ have equal infima over monic multiples.
Otherwise only the first row survives: $b_2$ can vanish and the mass be
linear for pairs within any fixed angle of the imaginary axis.  An
Archimedean Newton polygon restores rows and mass up to constants at moduli
separated by a factor $9$; with several roots per annulus, separation $81$
gives a sharp mass bound charging each root once per annulus at or below
it, and the real-root cross terms fail.  Real roots of both signs charge one root
of each sign per excluded coefficient, with sharp mass constant $\frac14$.
At distinct Gaussian integers of modulus at least $\rho>2$ above the
axis, integer multiples split into blocks paying $\log(\rho/2)$ per unit of
degree.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Polynomial multiples; coefficient mass; complex
roots; Newton polygon; Gaussian integers.

\noindent\textbf{2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.} Primary 11C08; Secondary 30C15, 26C10.

\section{Introduction}

For a nonzero $F(x)=\sum_jf_jx^j$ of degree $D$ write
\[
  \Lambda(F)=\sum_{j:f_j\ne0}\log^+|f_j|,\qquad \log^+x=\max(0,\log x),
\]
for its logarithmic coefficient mass, and $b_k(F)$ for the $k$-th largest of
the $D$ nonleading magnitudes $|f_j|$, $j<D$, counted with repetition and
zeros included ($b_k(F)=0$ for $k>D$).

For real roots
$2\le r_1\le\cdots\le r_L$, repetitions allowed, the companion
paper~\cite{coefficient-mass} proves that every nonzero real or complex
multiple $F$ of $\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order}
  b_k(F)\ \ge\ |f_D|\prod_{i=k}^L(r_i-1)\quad(1\le k\le L),
  \qquad
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (L+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{i=1}^Li\log(r_i-1)
\end{equation}
\cite[Theorem~3.2, Corollaries~3.3 and~3.4]{coefficient-mass}, uniformly in
the degree; we call the first bound at a given $k$ the row $k$.  The row
$k=1$ needs only $r_1>1$.  This paper asks what remains of
\eqref{eq:order} when the prescribed roots are complex.

\paragraph{Main results.}  Purely imaginary pairs transfer exactly: a pair
$\pm ic$ is worth one real root $c^2$, with the same infima of every $b_k$
and of $\Lambda$ over monic multiples (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer},
Corollary~\ref{cor:imag}).  At arbitrary angles only the first row survives:
it holds at every root set, while $b_2$ can vanish and the mass be linear in
the number of pairs (Propositions~\ref{prop:rowone} and~\ref{prop:noangle}).
Moduli separated by a factor $9$ restore the rows and the mass up to
constants (Corollary~\ref{cor:separated}); with several roots per annulus,
separation $81$ gives a mass bound, sharp to leading order, that charges
each root once for every annulus at or below it, where the cross terms of
\eqref{eq:order} fail (Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap},
Proposition~\ref{prop:annulisharp}).  Real roots of both signs give a mass
bound with the sharp leading constant $\frac14$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:bothsignsmass}, Proposition~\ref{prop:bothsignssharp}).
At distinct Gaussian-integer roots of modulus at least $\rho>2$ above the
axis, integer multiples split into blocks paying $\log(\rho/2)$ per unit of
degree (Theorem~\ref{thm:gausscharge}).

Reduced height and reduced length, the least height and length of a
normalized multiple, have been studied for real and complex
coefficients~\cite{dj,schinzel-complex}, and power series with restricted
coefficients and a root on a given ray in~\cite{bbbp-ray}.  The first row
below, $b_1(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_j(|\alpha_j|-1)$, is the complex analogue of the
bound $H(P)\ge|P(1)|$ on the reduced height $H(P)$ (the infimum of the
heights of the multiples $PQ$, $Q$ monic) obtained in~\cite{dj} for real roots
greater than one under an extra sign condition~\cite[p.~333]{dj}; that
condition is removed by the $u=0$ clause of~\cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass}.
The two bounds agree at such roots, but at complex
roots $|P(1)|$ is not a lower bound for $H(P)$: $P=x^2+\rho^2$ has $H(P)\le\rho^2$ (take $Q=1$),
while $|P(1)|=1+\rho^2$ and $\prod_j(|\alpha_j|-1)=(\rho-1)^2$.

\paragraph{Exact reductions.}  If the prescribed factor has the form
$R(x^m)$, multisection reduces every multiple to a multiple of $R$ with a
subset of the coefficients (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}).  In particular a
purely imaginary pair $\pm ic$ is worth exactly one real root $c^2$, and all
the real-root results transfer (Corollary~\ref{cor:imag}).  A rotation
reduces roots on a common ray to real roots at their moduli, which gives the
rows (Corollary~\ref{cor:ray}).

\paragraph{Arbitrary angles.}  The first row survives at every root set
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rowone}), but nothing more does in general: pairs
within any fixed angle of the imaginary axis admit multiples with $b_2=0$
and mass linear in the number of pairs (Proposition~\ref{prop:noangle}).

\paragraph{Separated moduli.}  Moduli separated by a factor $9$ restore the
rows and the mass up to constants (Corollary~\ref{cor:separated}), and with
several roots per annulus a separation $81$ between annuli gives a mass
bound charging each root once for every annulus at or below it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap}).  Both come from an Archimedean Newton polygon
(Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}).  In detail, for $K_s$ roots with moduli in $[T_s,U_s]$,
$1\le s\le S$, where $T_1>3$ and $T_s>81U_{s-1}$, the central index charges a
root once for every annulus at or below it,
$\sum_ssK_s\log(T_s/2)-O(S)$ in all for multiples with $|f_D|\ge1$, attained for suitable roots as $\min_sT_s\to\infty$; the cross terms $K_uK_s\log T_s$ of \eqref{eq:order} fail
(Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli},
Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap},
Proposition~\ref{prop:annulisharp}).  A central index at radius $r$ has at
least $n$ positions above it once $n$ roots have modulus above
$\frac73nr$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}), and the rows of
Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli} need a separation growing with the number of
roots above: at the separation $n_a$, $(x-q)(x+Kq)^K$ has $b_2$ below the
row by a factor that tends to $0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}), while
they hold at the separation $\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})$, where
$3\rho_{n_a}<7n_a$ (Corollaries~\ref{cor:fewabove} and~\ref{cor:rowsall}).
For two annuli the separation $n_2+1$ suffices, as a truncation of
$\prod(1-x/\beta)^{-1}$ stays away from $0$ below $T/n$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}, Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo},
Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}); for more annuli the truncation at a zero of
the annulus below, together with central indices further down, gives
$n_a+9$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rowsk}), but the row $3$ needs both separations
to grow with the number of roots above (Proposition~\ref{prop:rowslower}).
Sharpening the constant, for two annuli the least separation for the
rows exceeds $n_2$ by between $n_22^{-n_2-1}$ and $3n_22^{-n_2}$ once
$n_2\ge4$, and is at most $4$ when $n_2\le3$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:twobelow},
Corollary~\ref{cor:twofixed}); with one root above the gap it lies between
$\frac43$ and $\frac43+\frac2{3T_1}$, as eliminating one position between a
zero below the gap and one above costs no factor $\lambda$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:twozeros}, Corollary~\ref{cor:twonear}).
For more annuli, eliminating the coefficient charged below with a zero of
the next annulus down gives $n_a+1$ once $n_a\ge30$, and for large $n_a$ the
additive constant is exactly $\frac12$: $n_a+c$ suffices for every
$c>\frac12$ and fails for every $c<\frac12$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rowspair},
Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsplusone}, Proposition~\ref{prop:rowslower}).  At a
lower zero $\alpha$ the second row is at least
$\lambda(1-1/|\alpha|)^2$ times its charge, $\lambda=1-n|\alpha|/T$, with
the constant $1$ attained as $|\alpha|\to\infty$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rowstwolarge}).  The mass bound fails with
$T_2=(2+\frac1{18915})U_1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:gaptwo}), with two roots
above the gap, where the separation $\frac52$ suffices, and
$2+\frac2{T_1}$, so that the least separation tends to $2$ as $T_1$ grows
(Corollary~\ref{cor:twonear}); for two annuli with many roots above the
gap, a fixed separation below $81$ would have to charge three nonleading
positions.

\paragraph{Both signs.}  For real roots of both signs, Rolle's theorem on
each half-line charges one root of each sign per excluded coefficient
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bothsigns}); the resulting mass bound has the sharp
leading constant $\frac14$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:bothsignsmass},
Proposition~\ref{prop:bothsignssharp}).

\paragraph{Gaussian roots.}  At distinct Gaussian-integer roots in the upper
half-plane, an integer multiple whose coefficients below a position are
small has its part from that position on vanishing at the roots as well
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gausscarry}).  So it splits into blocks
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussblocks}), each paying $\log(\rho_{\min}/2)$ per unit of
degree, whatever the degree (Theorem~\ref{thm:gausscharge}).  Roots in a
sector, where the argument principle along a ray next to the sector gives
many real zeros of a real polynomial dominated coefficientwise by the
multiple, are treated in~\cite{coefficient-mass-sectors}, which uses
Theorem~\ref{thm:gausscharge} for integer multiples of large degree.

\paragraph{Open questions.}  Two questions are left open.  First, what the
least fixed separation of the annuli for the mass is: it lies between
$2+\frac1{18915}$ and $81$ (and at most $\max(4,n_2+1)$ for two annuli
with $n_2$ roots in the upper one, $\frac52$ if $n_2\le2$), and whether the separation $9$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli},
item~2, suffices is open (Section~\ref{sec:annuli}).  Second, whether
$\Lambda(F)\ge cK^2\log R$ holds for every integer multiple $F$ at $K$
distinct Gaussian-integer roots of modulus at least $R$ in the upper
half-plane within a sector of fixed half-width $\delta<\pi/2$.  For real multiples it
fails already at $x^{2M}+\rho^{2M}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:noangle}); for
integer multiples at Gaussian roots in such a sector the bound
of~\cite{coefficient-mass-sectors} is $(\frac\pi\delta-o(1))K\log R$, linear
in $K$, and root sets symmetric under $z\mapsto-\bar z$ or under the units
show there that such a $c$ is at most $1$, and at most $\frac12$ when
$\delta>\pi/4$.

\paragraph{Conventions.}
All logarithms are natural.  For $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ of degree $D$ we write
$F^*(t)=t^DF(1/t)=\sum_{d=0}^Df_{D-d}t^d$ for the reciprocal polynomial.
Coefficients of a polynomial named by a capital letter, when used, carry the
lowercase letter: $F=\sum_jf_jx^j$, $B=\sum_ib_ix^i$.  With an argument,
$b_k(\cdot)$ is always the $k$-th largest nonleading magnitude, so $b_k(B)$
and the coefficient $b_k$ of $B$ differ.
References of the form \cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass} are to the
companion paper.

\section{Exact reductions}\label{sec:exact}

\begin{lemma}[Multisection]\label{lem:multisection}
Let $m\ge1$, let $R\in\mathbb C[y]$ with $R(0)\ne0$, and put $P(x)=R(x^m)$.  Write
a polynomial $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ as
\[
  F(x)=\sum_{\varepsilon=0}^{m-1}x^\varepsilon E_\varepsilon(x^m),
  \qquad E_\varepsilon(y)=\sum_{i\ge0}f_{mi+\varepsilon}\,y^i .
\]
If $P$ divides $F$, then $R$ divides every $E_\varepsilon$.  If $F$ and $R$
are real, so are the $E_\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/m}$.  Since $P(\zeta^kx)=R(\zeta^{km}x^m)=P(x)$, the
polynomial $P$ divides $F(\zeta^kx)$ for every $k$, hence divides
\[
  \frac1m\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\zeta^{-k\varepsilon}F(\zeta^kx)
  =x^\varepsilon E_\varepsilon(x^m).
\]
As $R(0)\ne0$, $P(0)\ne0$, so $x^\varepsilon$ is coprime to $P$ and
$R(x^m)\mid E_\varepsilon(x^m)$.  Divide in $\mathbb C[y]$:
$E_\varepsilon=RQ+S$ with $\deg S<\deg R$.  Then $R(x^m)$ divides
$S(x^m)$, whose degree $m\deg S$ is smaller than $m\deg R$, so $S=0$.
Reality of $E_\varepsilon$ is clear from its coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Multisection transfer]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $m$, $R$ and $P=R(x^m)$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:multisection}, and let
$F$ be a nonzero multiple of $P$ of degree $D$.  For the residue
$\varepsilon\equiv D\pmod m$, the polynomial $E_\varepsilon$ is a multiple of
$R$ of degree $(D-\varepsilon)/m$ with leading coefficient $f_D$, and
\[
  b_k(F)\ge b_k(E_\varepsilon)\quad(k\ge1),\qquad
  \Lambda(F)\ge\Lambda(E_\varepsilon).
\]
Conversely, for every multiple $G$ of $R$ the polynomial $G(x^m)$ is a
multiple of $P$ with the same nonzero coefficients.  Consequently, for
$\Phi=b_k$ (any fixed $k$) and for $\Phi=\Lambda$,
\[
  \inf\{\Phi(F):F\text{ monic},\ P\mid F\}
  =\inf\{\Phi(G):G\text{ monic},\ R\mid G\},
\]
over complex multiples, and also over real multiples when $R$ is real.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:multisection}, $R\mid E_\varepsilon$.  The top coefficient
of $F$ sits at $D=m\cdot\frac{D-\varepsilon}m+\varepsilon$, so it is the
coefficient of $y^{(D-\varepsilon)/m}$ in $E_\varepsilon$, and no higher
coefficient of $E_\varepsilon$ is nonzero.  The nonleading coefficients of
$E_\varepsilon$ are the $f_j$ with $j\equiv\varepsilon$, $j<D$: a
sub-multiset of the nonleading coefficients of $F$.  The $k$-th largest
element of a sub-multiset is at most the $k$-th largest of the whole, and
$\Lambda$ is a sum of nonnegative terms over coefficients; this gives the
two inequalities.  Monicity passes from $F$ to $E_\varepsilon$, which gives
$\ge$ between the infima.  For the converse, $R\mid G$ gives
$R(x^m)\mid G(x^m)$, and $G(x^m)$ has the coefficients of $G$ spread out
with zeros between them, so $b_k$ and $\Lambda$ are unchanged, and
$G(x^m)$ is monic when $G$ is.  This gives $\le$.
\end{proof}

The case $m=2$, $R(y)=\prod_j(y+c_j^2)$ is the case of purely imaginary
conjugate pairs; the sign change $y\mapsto-y$ turns $R$ into a product of
linear factors at the positive roots $c_j^2$ without changing any
coefficient magnitude.

\begin{corollary}[Purely imaginary pairs]\label{cor:imag}
Let $c_1\le\cdots\le c_K$ be positive reals, repetitions allowed, put
$P_{\rm im}=\prod_{j=1}^K(x^2+c_j^2)$ and
$P_{\rm re}=\prod_{j=1}^K(y-c_j^2)$, and let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be a nonzero
multiple of $P_{\rm im}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $c_1^2\ge2$, then for $1\le k\le K$,
\[
  b_k(F)\ \ge\ |f_D|\prod_{j=k}^K(c_j^2-1),
\]
and at least $k$ positions $j<D$ with $j\equiv D\pmod2$ carry magnitudes
$|f_j|$ at least this bound.
The row $k=1$ holds for $c_1>1$.
\item If $c_1^2\ge2$ and $|f_D|\ge1$, then
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (K+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{j=1}^Kj\log(c_j^2-1).
\]
\item For $\Phi=b_k$ and $\Phi=\Lambda$, the infimum of $\Phi$ over monic
(real or complex) multiples of $P_{\rm im}$ equals its infimum over monic
multiples of $P_{\rm re}$.  In particular, with $c_1^2\ge2$ and $K\ge2$,
\[
  \inf_{Q\text{ monic}}\Lambda(P_{\rm im}Q)\ \asymp\
  K^2+\sum_{j=1}^Kj\log(c_j^2-1)
\]
with the absolute constants of \cite[Corollary~4.3]{coefficient-mass};
$\Lambda(F)\ge K^2/80$ for monic $F$ when $K\ge8$; and for $K=2$ and
$(c_1^2,c_2^2)=(2,3)$ the row $k=2$ of item~1 is sharp:
$\inf_{Q\text{ monic}}b_2(P_{\rm im}Q)=c_2^2-1=2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} with $m=2$ and $R(y)=\prod_j(y+c_j^2)$;
$R(0)\ne0$.  The multiple $E_\varepsilon$ of $R$ has leading coefficient
$f_D$, and $\tilde E(y)=E_\varepsilon(-y)$ is a multiple of $P_{\rm re}$
with leading coefficient $\pm f_D$ and the same coefficient magnitudes.  \cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass}, with \cite[Corollary~3.3]{coefficient-mass} for complex $F$, applied to
$\tilde E$ at the roots $c_j^2\ge2$ gives item~1, since
$b_k(F)\ge b_k(\tilde E)$ and the positions of $\tilde E$ are those
$j\equiv D\pmod 2$ of $F$; the clause $c_1>1$ is the clause $r_1>1$ for the row $k=1$ of
\cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass}.  Item~2 is \cite[Corollary~3.4]{coefficient-mass} applied to $\tilde E$, together with
$\Lambda(F)\ge\Lambda(\tilde E)$.  Item~3 is the equality of infima in
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, composed with the magnitude-preserving
bijection $G(y)\mapsto\pm G(-y)$ between monic multiples of $R$ and of
$P_{\rm re}$; the three quoted statements are \cite[Corollary~4.3]{coefficient-mass},
\cite[Proposition~4.2]{coefficient-mass} and \cite[Proposition~4.4]{coefficient-mass} at
the roots $c_j^2$.  These are stated for real multiples; they hold for complex
ones because, as in \cite[Corollary~3.3]{coefficient-mass}, a monic complex
multiple $G$ of $P_{\rm re}$ dominates coefficientwise the monic real multiple
$\operatorname{Re}G$, and $b_k$ and $\Lambda$ do not increase under such
domination.
\end{proof}

Thus a purely imaginary pair $\pm ic$ is worth exactly one real root $c^2$:
the pair has two roots of modulus $c$, and the mass charges $\log c^2$ once
at its rank.  This is the right count.  By item~3 the product
$P_{\rm im}$ itself is within $O(K^2)$ of the infimum
(\cite[Corollary~3.7]{coefficient-mass}), and $\Lambda(P_{\rm im})=\Lambda(P_{\rm re})$; so a
bound of the form $\sum_{i\le2K}i\log\rho_{(i)}$, where
$\rho_{(1)}\le\cdots\le\rho_{(2K)}$ are the moduli of the $2K$ roots
counted separately, which would be about twice as large, is false for
$P_{\rm im}$ once $\min_jc_j$ is large: it exceeds the bound
$\Lambda(P_{\rm im})\le\sum_j\log\binom Kj+\sum_j2j\log c_j$ by at least
$K^2\log c_1-\sum_j\log\binom Kj$.

The same proof applies to every factor of the form $R(x^m)$ with $R$ real
and all roots of $R$ of one sign: the real roots $\pm r$ in pairs
($R=\prod(y-r_j^2)$), or, for fixed $m$, all $m$-th roots of real $s_j$ of
one sign.  One
more reduction needs no multisection.

\begin{corollary}[Roots on a common ray]\label{cor:ray}
Let $\theta\in\R$ and $\rho_1\le\cdots\le\rho_K$ with $\rho_1\ge2$, and let
$F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be a nonzero multiple of $\prod_j(x-\rho_je^{i\theta})$.  Then
$b_k(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_{j\ge k}(\rho_j-1)$ for $1\le k\le K$.  In
particular this holds for a real $F$ divisible by
$\prod_j(x^2-2\rho_j\cos\theta\,x+\rho_j^2)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$G(x)=F(e^{i\theta}x)$ has coefficients $f_je^{ij\theta}$, of the same
magnitudes, and is divisible by $\prod_j(x-\rho_j)$; apply
\cite[Corollary~3.3]{coefficient-mass}.
\end{proof}

A common ray charges each pair once, with $\log(\rho-1)$ in place of
$\log(\rho^2-1)$; the imaginary axis is special because it carries both
members of each pair.


\section{Arbitrary angles}\label{sec:angles}

\begin{proposition}[The first row at arbitrary roots]\label{prop:rowone}
Let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_N\in\mathbb C$, repetitions allowed, with
$\rho_j=|\alpha_j|$, and let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be a nonzero multiple of
$\prod_j(x-\alpha_j)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If every $\rho_j>1$, then $b_1(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_{j=1}^N(\rho_j-1)$.
\item For every $0<r<1$,
$b_1(F)\ge|f_D|\,\frac{1-r}{r}\bigl(r^N\mathcal M-1\bigr)$, where
$\mathcal M=\prod_j\max(1,\rho_j)$; with $r=N/(N+1)$,
$b_1(F)\ge|f_D|\,(\mathcal M/e-1)/N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $F^*(t)=t^DF(1/t)=\sum_{d=0}^Df_{D-d}t^d$ and $\beta_j=1/\alpha_j$
where $\alpha_j\ne0$.

\emph{Item 1.}  Here every $\alpha_j\ne0$, and if $(x-\alpha)^\mu\mid F$
then $F^*(t)=(1-\alpha t)^\mu\,t^{D-\mu}H(1/t)$ with $F=(x-\alpha)^\mu H$, so
$\chi(t)=\prod_j(t-\beta_j)$ divides $F^*$.  Put
$B(s)=\prod_j(1-\beta_js)=s^N\chi(1/s)$, let $c=-[s^N]B$, and let
$v_d=[s^d]V$ for $V=(B+cs^N)/B=1+cs^N/B$.  The numerator $A=B+cs^N$ has
degree at most $N-1$, so $BV=A$ gives, for every $n\ge N$,
$\sum_{k=0}^N[s^k]B\;v_{n-k}=0$, that is,
$\sum_{i=0}^N\chi_iv_{m'+i}=0$ for every $m'\ge0$, where
$\chi=\sum_i\chi_it^i$.  Writing $F^*=\chi h$,
\[
  \sum_{d}v_df_{D-d}
  =\sum_{m'}h_{m'}\sum_i\chi_iv_{m'+i}=0 .
\]
Since $v_0=1$, $|f_D|\le b_1(F)\sum_{d\ge1}|v_d|$.  Now
$|c|=\prod_j|\beta_j|=\prod_j\rho_j^{-1}$, and the coefficients of $1/B$ are
dominated in modulus by those of $\prod_j(1-|\beta_j|s)^{-1}$, whose sum is
$\prod_j(1-\rho_j^{-1})^{-1}$.  Hence
$\sum_{d\ge1}|v_d|\le\prod_j\rho_j^{-1}(1-\rho_j^{-1})^{-1}
=\prod_j(\rho_j-1)^{-1}$.

\emph{Item 2.}  $F^*(0)=f_D\ne0$, and $F^*$ vanishes at every $\beta_j$
(counted with multiplicity, as in item 1).  Jensen's formula on $|t|\le r$
gives
\[
  \log\max_{|t|=r}|F^*|\ \ge\ \log|f_D|+\sum_{j:\rho_j>1/r}\log(r\rho_j)
  \ \ge\ \log|f_D|+\sum_{j}\log\bigl(r\max(1,\rho_j)\bigr),
\]
since each omitted or added term is $\le0$ on the right.  On the other side
$\max_{|t|=r}|F^*|\le|f_D|+b_1(F)\sum_{d\ge1}r^d
=|f_D|+b_1(F)\,r/(1-r)$.  Rearranging gives the first bound; for
$r=N/(N+1)$, $(1-r)/r=1/N$ and $r^N=(1+1/N)^{-N}\ge e^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

For real roots $r_j>1$ item 1 is the row $k=1$ of \cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass}.  Both items
are of the size of the Mahler measure: they charge each root once, as
$\log\rho_j$, and do not give a second large coefficient.  At arbitrary
angles nothing more is true.

\begin{proposition}[No angle-uniform rows beyond the first]
\label{prop:noangle}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $M\ge3$ be odd, $\rho>1$, and $0<\delta<\pi/2$.  The monic
polynomial $x^{2M}+\rho^{2M}$ is divisible by the product of the
$K=2\lfloor\delta M/\pi\rfloor+1$ conjugate pairs of modulus $\rho$ with
arguments $\pm(\frac\pi2+t\frac\pi M)$ for the integers $|t|\le\delta M/\pi$, all within
$\delta$ of the imaginary axis; yet
\[
  b_2(x^{2M}+\rho^{2M})=0,\qquad
  \Lambda(x^{2M}+\rho^{2M})=2M\log\rho\le\frac\pi\delta(K+1)\log\rho .
\]
\item For $m\ge2$ and real $a\ge1$, the polynomial $x^{2m}+x+a$ has no real
root, so its roots form $m$ conjugate pairs; all of them have moduli in
$[\rho/3,3\rho]$, where $\rho=a^{1/(2m)}$, and $b_2=1$,
$\Lambda=\log a=2m\log\rho$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, no bound $b_2(F)\ge\phi(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_K)>0$ in terms of
the moduli of the prescribed pairs alone, not even for pairs within a fixed
angle $\delta$ of the imaginary axis, and no bound
$\Lambda(F)\ge c\,K^2\log\rho$ for $K$ pairs of modulus $\rho$ within a
fixed angle $\delta$, can hold for all monic multiples.  Item~2 gives
the same linear mass without equal moduli or symmetric angles: $m$ pairs
with moduli within a factor $3$ of $\rho$, and a multiple with $b_2=1$ and
$\Lambda=2m\log\rho$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
1.  The roots of $x^{2M}+\rho^{2M}$ are $\rho e^{i\pi(2l+1)/(2M)}$; for
$M$ odd, $l=(M-1)/2+t$ gives the argument $\frac\pi2+t\frac\pi M$, and the
conjugate root has the negated argument.  The integers $t$ with
$|t|\le\delta M/\pi$ number $K$.  The only nonleading nonzero coefficient
is $\rho^{2M}$, so $b_2=0$ and $\Lambda=2M\log\rho$.  Finally
$K\ge2\delta M/\pi-1$ gives $2M\le\pi(K+1)/\delta$.

2.  For real $x$ with $|x|\ge1$, $x^{2m}+x\ge|x|^{2m}-|x|\ge0$, and for
$|x|<1$, $x^{2m}+x>-1$; so $x^{2m}+x+a>0$ when $a\ge1$.  The coefficients are
$1,1,a$.  The point $(1,\log1)=(1,0)$ lies on or below the chord from
$(0,\log a)$ to $(2m,0)$, whose height at $1$ is $(1-\frac1{2m})\log a\ge0$,
so the only tropical root (defined before Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}) is
$\log\rho=(\log a)/(2m)$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~2, puts every
root in $[\rho/3,3\rho]$.

For the final sentence, item 1 with $\delta M\ge\pi$, so $K\ge3$, has $b_2=0$ while every
$\rho_j=\rho$ is fixed, and letting $M\to\infty$ at fixed $\delta$ gives
$K\to\infty$ with $\Lambda=O_\delta(K\log\rho)$.
\end{proof}

What the imaginary axis provides in Corollary~\ref{cor:imag} is therefore
not closeness of the angles but exact membership, which makes the
prescribed factor a polynomial in $x^2$.  Separated moduli, on the other
hand, do help, through the Archimedean Newton polygon.  For a nonzero
$F=\sum_if_ix^i\in\mathbb C[x]$ of degree $D$ and order $e$ at $0$, let
$\varphi$ be the least concave function on $[e,D]$ with
$\varphi(i)\ge\log|f_i|$ whenever $f_i\ne0$.  Its vertices
$D=v_0>v_1>\cdots>v_J=e$ are among these points, so $\varphi(v_t)=\log|f_{v_t}|$.
The edge joining $v_s$ to $v_{s-1}$ has length $\ell_s=v_{s-1}-v_s\ge1$ and
slope $-\sigma_s$; by concavity $\sigma_1>\sigma_2>\cdots>\sigma_J$, and
\[
  \log|f_{v_t}|=\log|f_D|+\sum_{s\le t}\ell_s\sigma_s\qquad(0\le t\le J).
\]
We call $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_J$ the \emph{tropical roots} of $F$.  For
$u\in\R$ the vertex $v_s$ with $\sigma_{s+1}<u<\sigma_s$ (where
$\sigma_0=+\infty$ and $\sigma_{J+1}=-\infty$) is the one maximizing
$\log|f_i|+iu$; we call it \emph{active} at $u$.

\begin{theorem}[Archimedean Newton polygon]\label{thm:archnewton}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero, with tropical roots $\sigma_1>\cdots>\sigma_J$:
the edges of the least concave majorant $\varphi$ of the points
$(i,\log|f_i|)$, $f_i\ne0$, have slopes $-\sigma_1,\ldots,-\sigma_J$.  For
$u\in\R$ other than a tropical root, the vertex \emph{active} at $u$ is the
position maximizing $\log|f_i|+iu$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $u\in\R$ satisfy $|u-\sigma_s|>\log3$ for every $s$, let $v$ be the
vertex active at $u$, and $r=e^u$.  Then
$|f_v|r^v>\sum_{i\ne v}|f_i|r^i$.  Hence $F$ has no root on $|z|=r$ and
exactly $v$ roots, counted with multiplicity, in $|z|<r$.
\item Every nonzero root $\beta$ of $F$ satisfies
$\bigl|\log|\beta|-\sigma_s\bigr|\le\log3$ for some $s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
1.  Let $v=v_s$, so $\sigma_{s+1}<u<\sigma_s$, and put $d_+=\sigma_s-u$ and
$d_-=u-\sigma_{s+1}$, both larger than $\log3$ (and infinite when $s=0$,
respectively $s=J$).  A concave function lies below the line through any of
its edges, so for $i>v$ the edge of slope $-\sigma_s$ gives
$\log|f_i|\le\varphi(i)\le\varphi(v)-\sigma_s(i-v)$, that is,
$|f_i|r^i\le|f_v|r^ve^{-(i-v)d_+}$; likewise the edge of slope $-\sigma_{s+1}$
gives $|f_i|r^i\le|f_v|r^ve^{-(v-i)d_-}$ for $i<v$.  Summing over the
distinct positive integers $|i-v|$,
\[
  \sum_{i\ne v}|f_i|r^i\ \le\ |f_v|r^v\Bigl(\frac1{e^{d_+}-1}+\frac1{e^{d_-}-1}\Bigr)
  \ <\ |f_v|r^v\Bigl(\frac12+\frac12\Bigr).
\]
So $|F(z)|\ge|f_v|r^v-\sum_{i\ne v}|f_i|r^i>0$ on $|z|=r$, and by Rouch\'e's
theorem $F$ and $f_vz^v$ have the same number of zeros in $|z|<r$, namely $v$.

2.  If $|\log|\beta|-\sigma_s|>\log3$ for every $s$, item~1 at
$u=\log|\beta|$ shows $F(\beta)\ne0$.
\end{proof}

Item~2 is the Archimedean form of the Newton polygon theorem: up to the
factor $3$, the moduli of the roots can only sit at the tropical roots.
Bounds of this kind are classical: they go back to Hadamard and to
Ostrowski's analysis of the Graeffe method~\cite{ostrowski}; item~1 is
Pellet's theorem~\cite{marden} at the active vertex; and the tropical roots
and log-majorization of root moduli by them are developed
in~\cite{akian-gaubert-sharify}.  We include the short proof to fix the
constant $3$.
Unlike the non-Archimedean case, the roots of modulus near $e^{\sigma_s}$ need not
number $\ell_s$; $(x-1)^D$ has all its roots on $|z|=1$ but
tropical roots $\log\frac{i+1}{D-i}$ spread over $[-\log D,\log D]$.
Separated roots still force separated vertices, which is all the mass needs.

\begin{corollary}[Separated moduli]\label{cor:separated}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, and let
$\rho_1\le\cdots\le\rho_K$ be moduli of nonzero roots of $F$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\rho_1\ge3$ and $\rho_{j+1}>9\rho_j$ for $1\le j<K$, there are
positions $y_1<\cdots<y_K<D$ with
\[
  |f_{y_j}|\ \ge\ |f_D|\prod_{i=j}^K\frac{\rho_i}3 .
\]
Hence $b_k(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_{i=k}^K(\rho_i/3)$ for $1\le k\le K$, and, if
$|f_D|\ge1$,
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (K+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{j=1}^Kj\log\frac{\rho_j}3 .
\]
\item If moreover each $\rho_j$ is the modulus of two roots of $F$, counted
with multiplicity (for instance $\beta_j$ and $\bar\beta_j$ with $F$ real and
$\beta_j\notin\R$), and $\rho_1>27$ and $\rho_{j+1}>729\rho_j$, then
$b_k(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_{i=k}^K(\rho_i^2/729)$ for $1\le k\le K$, and, if
$|f_D|\ge1$,
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (K+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{j=1}^Kj\log\frac{\rho_j^2}{729} .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
1.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~2, choose for each $j$ an index
$s(j)$ with $|\sigma_{s(j)}-\log\rho_j|\le\log3$.  Since
$\log\rho_{j+1}-\log\rho_j>2\log3$,
\[
  \sigma_{s(j+1)}\ge\log\rho_{j+1}-\log3>\log\rho_j+\log3\ge\sigma_{s(j)},
\]
so $s(1)>s(2)>\cdots>s(K)\ge1$, and $y_j=v_{s(j)}$ are increasing positions
below $D$.  In
$\log|f_{y_j}|=\log|f_D|+\sum_{s\le s(j)}\ell_s\sigma_s$ every term has
$\ell_s\ge1$ and $\sigma_s\ge\sigma_{s(j)}\ge\log(\rho_j/3)\ge0$; keeping only
the terms $s=s(i)$, $i\ge j$, gives
$\log|f_{y_j}|\ge\log|f_D|+\sum_{i\ge j}\log(\rho_i/3)$.  Every factor
$\rho_i/3$ is at least $1$, so the $k$ positions $y_1,\ldots,y_k$ all carry
magnitudes at least $|f_D|\prod_{i\ge k}(\rho_i/3)$, which bounds $b_k(F)$.
For the mass, $\Lambda(F)\ge\log^+|f_D|+\sum_j\log^+|f_{y_j}|$, and
$\sum_j\sum_{i\ge j}\log(\rho_i/3)=\sum_ii\log(\rho_i/3)$.

2.  Fix $c$ with $3\log3<c<\log\rho_1$ and $2c<\log(\rho_{j+1}/\rho_j)$ for
every $j$, and let $W_j=[\log\rho_j-c,\log\rho_j+c]$.  These windows are
disjoint, lie in $(0,\infty)$, and $W_{j+1}$ lies above $W_j$.  We claim that
the tropical roots in $W_j$ have total edge length
$\sum_{\sigma_s\in W_j}\ell_s\ge2$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~2,
$W_j$ contains a tropical root $\sigma_s$ with $|\sigma_s-\log\rho_j|\le\log3$.
If it contains another one, the claim holds.  Otherwise take
$0<\varepsilon<c-3\log3$ and $u_\pm=\sigma_s\pm(\log3+\varepsilon)$.  Both are
farther than $\log3$ from $\sigma_s$, and every other tropical root lies
outside $W_j$, at distance more than $c-(2\log3+\varepsilon)>\log3$ from
$u_\pm$, because $|u_\pm-\log\rho_j|\le2\log3+\varepsilon$.  No tropical root
other than $\sigma_s$ lies in $[u_-,u_+]\subset W_j$, so the vertices active
at $u_+$ and $u_-$ are $v_{s-1}$ and $v_s$, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~1, $F$ has exactly
$v_{s-1}-v_s=\ell_s$ roots in $e^{u_-}<|z|<e^{u_+}$.  The two roots of modulus
$\rho_j$ are among them, as $|\log\rho_j-\sigma_s|\le\log3$; so $\ell_s\ge2$.

Now let $\sigma_{s(j)}$ be the smallest tropical root in $W_j$ and
$y_j=v_{s(j)}$.  In $\log|f_{y_j}|=\log|f_D|+\sum_{s\le s(j)}\ell_s\sigma_s$
every $\sigma_s$ is at least $\sigma_{s(j)}>0$, and the sum contains every
tropical root in $W_i$ for $i\ge j$; each of these is at least
$\log\rho_i-c$.  By the claim,
$\log|f_{y_j}|\ge\log|f_D|+2\sum_{i\ge j}(\log\rho_i-c)$.  As in item~1,
$b_k(F)\ge|f_D|\prod_{i\ge k}(\rho_i^2e^{-2c})$ and
$\Lambda(F)\ge(K+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_jj\log(\rho_j^2e^{-2c})$.  These hold for
every $c$ in a nonempty interval with infimum $3\log3$; letting
$c\downarrow3\log3$ gives the stated bounds, as $e^{6\log3}=729$.

\end{proof}

So when the prescribed moduli grow by a factor $9$ at each step, every
complex multiple with $|f_D|\ge1$ pays $\sum_jj\log(\rho_j/3)$, one real root $\rho_j/3$ per
root, at every angle; with a factor $729$ a conjugate pair pays
$\log(\rho_j^2/729)$, which is the charge $\log(c_j^2-1)$ of
Corollary~\ref{cor:imag} up to the constant.  Proposition~\ref{prop:noangle}
shows that some separation is necessary; the constants $3$, $9$ and $729$ are
not optimized.

\section{Several annuli}\label{sec:annuli}

At complex roots in separated annuli the sharp worst-case mass bound charges
a root once for every annulus at or below it, and the cross terms between
annuli that \eqref{eq:order} has at real roots fail.  For real roots the
mass bound \eqref{eq:order} charges the $i$-th smallest
root $i$ times: once for itself and once for every smaller root.  Group $K_s$
real roots near $T_s$, with $T_1\ll\cdots\ll T_S$, and it reads
$\sum_s\bigl(K_1+\cdots+K_{s-1}+\frac{K_s+1}2\bigr)K_s\log T_s$ up to lower
order terms, with a square term $\frac12K_s^2\log T_s$ inside each group and a
cross term $K_uK_s\log T_s$ between the groups $u<s$.  At complex roots the
square term is false (Proposition~\ref{prop:noangle}), and so are the cross
terms: when the moduli of the $K_s$ roots
of the $s$-th group lie in $[T_s,U_s]$, with $T_1>3$ and $T_s>81U_{s-1}$, every multiple with $|f_D|\ge1$ has mass at least
$\sum_ssK_s\log(T_s/2)-S\log2$, and for suitable roots this is
attained to leading order (the ratio tends to $1$) as $\min_sT_s\to\infty$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap}, Proposition~\ref{prop:annulisharp}).
Corollary~\ref{cor:separated} is essentially the case $K_s=1$.

For a nonzero $F$ and $r>0$ we call a position $y$ maximizing $|f_i|r^i$ a
\emph{central index} at $r$, as in Wiman--Valiron theory~\cite{hayman}.  In the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton},
the central index at $r=e^u$ is the vertex active at $u$ when $u$ is not a
tropical root; at a tropical root it is any position $i$ of the corresponding
edge with $\log|f_i|=\varphi(i)$, its two vertices included.  Every central
index $y$ at $e^u$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centralabove}
  D-y\ \ge\ \sum_{\sigma_s>u}\ell_s .
\end{equation}
Indeed let $s$ be the largest index with
$\sigma_s>u$ ($s=0$ if there is none, and then $v_0=D$ and there is nothing
to prove); then $D-v_s=\sum_{t\le s}\ell_t$, and for $i>v_s$ the edge of
slope $-\sigma_s$ gives
$\log|f_i|+iu\le\log|f_{v_s}|+v_su-(\sigma_s-u)(i-v_s)<\log|f_{v_s}|+v_su$,
so $y\le v_s$.

\begin{lemma}[Central index]\label{lem:central}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, let $r>0$, and let $y$ be a
central index of $F$ at $r$.  For every sub-multiset $A$ of the zeros of $F$,
counted with multiplicity,
\[
  |f_y|\ >\ \frac{|f_D|}2\,r^{D-y-|A|}\prod_{\beta\in A}\max\Bigl(r,\frac{|\beta|}2\Bigr).
\]
In particular, if $r\ge1$ then $|f_y|>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A}\frac{|\beta|}{2r}$,
and if moreover $D-y\ge|A|$ then $|f_y|>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A}\frac{|\beta|}2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_D$ be the zeros of $F$ with multiplicity.  Jensen's
formula on $|z|=2r$ and the choice of $y$ give
\[
  |f_D|\prod_{l=1}^D\max(2r,|\beta_l|)\ \le\ \max_{|z|=2r}|F(z)|
  \ \le\ \sum_{i=0}^D|f_i|r^i2^i\ \le\ |f_y|r^y\sum_{i=0}^D2^i\ <\ |f_y|r^y2^{D+1}.
\]
Each of the $D-|A|$ factors $\max(2r,|\beta_l|)$ with $\beta_l\notin A$ is at
least $2r$, so
$|f_y|>|f_D|2^{-D-1}r^{-y}(2r)^{D-|A|}\prod_{\beta\in A}\max(2r,|\beta|)$,
which is the claim.  For $r\ge1$, $r^{D-y-|A|}\prod_A\max(r,|\beta|/2)\ge
r^{D-y}\prod_A|\beta|/(2r)\ge\prod_A|\beta|/(2r)$; if $D-y\ge|A|$, then
$r^{D-y-|A|}\ge1$ and $\max(r,|\beta|/2)\ge|\beta|/2$.
\end{proof}

At $r=1$ the exponent $D-y-|A|$ plays no role: the central coefficient at
radius $1$ carries every zero, as $\max(1,|\beta|/2)$, like the first row of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rowone}.  At a larger radius the lemma charges
$|\beta|/2$ only for as many zeros as there are positions above the central
index.  The next lemma supplies such positions.

\begin{lemma}[Counting at a distance]\label{lem:tropcount}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, let $A$ be a sub-multiset of
the zeros of $F$, counted with multiplicity, with $n=|A|\ge1$ and $|\beta|\ge
T>0$ for $\beta\in A$, and let $0<r<T$ and $\theta=r/T$.  If $0\le\kappa\le n$ and
\[
  (1-\theta)^{-n}\sum_{m=n-\kappa+1}^n\binom nm\theta^m\ <\ 1,
\]
then every central index $y$ of $F$ at $r$ satisfies $D-y\ge\kappa$.  In
particular $D-y\ge n$ if $T\ge3nr$, and $D-y\ge n/2$ if $T\ge6r$; and
$n-(D-y)\le\lfloor0.82\,n\rfloor$ if $T\ge3r$, and
$n-(D-y)\le\lfloor0.13\,n\rfloor$ if $T\ge27r$.  More precisely, let
$\theta_n$ be the root in $(0,1)$ of $(1+\theta)^n-1=(1-\theta)^n$ and
$\rho_n=1/\theta_n$; then $D-y\ge n$ if $T>\rho_nr$, where $\rho_1=2$,
$\rho_2=4$ and $\rho_n<\frac73n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $H=\prod_{\beta\in A}(x-\beta)=\sum_mh_mx^m$ and $P=F/H=\sum_jp_jx^j$, a
polynomial of degree $D-n$.  As $h_m=h_0(-1)^me_m(1/\beta)$ and $h_0\ne0$,
$|h_m|r^m\le|h_0|\binom nm\theta^m$.  In $\mathbb C[[x]]$,
$1/H=h_0^{-1}\prod_\beta(1-x/\beta)^{-1}=\sum_mq_mx^m$ with
$|q_m|\le|h_0|^{-1}\binom{n+m-1}mT^{-m}$, and $P=F\cdot(1/H)$, so
$p_j=\sum_{m\le j}f_{j-m}q_m$.  With $M=\max_{i\le D-n}|f_i|r^i$, for
$j\le D-n$,
\[
  |p_j|r^j\ \le\ \sum_m|f_{j-m}|r^{j-m}\,|q_m|r^m
  \ \le\ \frac M{|h_0|}\sum_m\binom{n+m-1}m\theta^m\ =\ \frac M{|h_0|}(1-\theta)^{-n};
\]
in particular $M>0$, as $P\ne0$.  For $i>D-\kappa$, $f_i=\sum_jp_jh_{i-j}$
over $j\le D-n$, so $i-j>n-\kappa$ and
\[
  |f_i|r^i\ \le\ \sum_j|p_j|r^j\,|h_{i-j}|r^{i-j}
  \ \le\ M(1-\theta)^{-n}\sum_{m>n-\kappa}\binom nm\theta^m\ <\ M .
\]
So no $i>D-\kappa$ is a central index.  If $T\ge3nr$ and $\kappa=n$, the sum
is $(1+\theta)^n-1\le e^{1/3}-1<\frac23\le1-n\theta\le(1-\theta)^n$.  If $T\ge6r$ and
$\kappa=\lceil n/2\rceil$, every $m>n-\kappa$ has $m\ge(n+1)/2$, so the left
side is at most $(1-\theta)^{-n}2^n\theta^{(n+1)/2}\le6^{-1/2}(12/(5\sqrt6))^n<1$.
For $\kappa=n$ the left side is $((1+\theta)^n-1)/(1-\theta)^n$, which
increases from $0$ to $\infty$ on $(0,1)$, so the hypothesis holds exactly
when $\theta<\theta_n$; for $n=1,2$ it reads $\theta<1-\theta$ and
$2\theta<1-2\theta$.  At $\theta=3/(7n)$,
$(1+\theta)^n-1\le e^{3/7}-1<\frac47\le1-n\theta\le(1-\theta)^n$, as
$e^3<(11/7)^7$; so $\theta_n>3/(7n)$.
For the bounds $\lfloor0.82\,n\rfloor$ and $\lfloor0.13\,n\rfloor$ let $0<c<1$, $M=\lfloor cn\rfloor+1>cn$ and
$\kappa=n-M+1=n-\lfloor cn\rfloor$.  For every $x\ge1$,
$\sum_{m\ge M}\binom nm\theta^m\le x^{-M}\sum_m\binom nm(\theta x)^m$, so the
left side is at most $x^{-M}\bigl((1+\theta x)/(1-\theta)\bigr)^n$, which
increases with $\theta$.  At $\theta=\frac13$, $x=16$, $c=0.82$ it is below
$(\frac{19}2\cdot16^{-0.82})^n<1$; at $\theta=\frac1{27}$, $x=4$, $c=0.13$ it
is below $(\frac{31}{26}\cdot4^{-0.13})^n<1$.
\end{proof}

The factor $3n$ is sharp up to the $3$: $(x-t)^K$ has $K$ zeros of modulus
$t$, and at $r=t/c$ its terms are $t^K\binom Kic^{-i}$, so for $c<K$ the term
$i=1$ beats the term $i=0$ and the central index is positive.  At a constant
distance only a fraction of the count survives: for $c\ge1$ the central
index of $(x-t)^K$ at $t/c$ is about $K/(c+1)$, with only about $cK/(c+1)$
positions above it.  The constant $\rho_1=2$ is sharp: every coefficient
of $x^D-\sum_{i<D}x^i$, $D\ge2$, has modulus $1$, so $D$ is a central index at $r=1$,
while $(x-1)(x^D-\sum_{i<D}x^i)=x^D(x-2)+1$ changes sign on
$[2-2^{1-D},2]$, giving a zero of modulus above $2-2^{1-D}$.

\begin{theorem}[Several annuli]\label{thm:annuli}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, let $S\ge1$, and let
$A_1,\ldots,A_S$ be nonempty sub-multisets of the zeros of $F$, counted with
multiplicity, the moduli in $A_s$ lying in $[T_s,U_s]$, where
\[
  T_1>3,\qquad T_a>9U_{a-1}\quad(2\le a\le S).
\]
Put $K_s=|A_s|$, $n_a=K_a+\cdots+K_S$ and $A_{\ge a}=A_a\cup\cdots\cup A_S$.
Let $r_1=1$ and $k_1=n_1$, and for $2\le a\le S$ choose $r_a$ with
$3U_{a-1}<r_a<T_a/3$, let $\kappa_a$ be the largest integer with
$3\kappa_ar_a\le T_a$, and put $k_a=\min\bigl(n_a,\max(\kappa_a,S-a+1)\bigr)$.
Then there are positions $y_1<y_2<\cdots<y_S<D$ with
\[
  |f_{y_a}|\ >\ B_a=\frac{|f_D|}2\,r_a^{k_a-n_a}\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge a}}\frac{|\beta|}2
  \qquad(1\le a\le S).
\]
Hence $b_k(F)>\min_{a\le k}B_a$ for $1\le k\le S$, and, if $|f_D|\ge1$,
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (S+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{s=1}^Ss\sum_{\beta\in A_s}\log\frac{|\beta|}2
  -\sum_{a=2}^S(n_a-k_a)\log r_a-S\log2 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $y_a$ be a central index of $F$ at $r_a$.  Since $r_a<T_a/3$,
$\kappa_a\ge1$.

\emph{Order.}  For $1\le j\le S$ pick $\beta\in A_j$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~2, some tropical root $\sigma^{(j)}$
satisfies $|\sigma^{(j)}-\log|\beta||\le\log3$, so
$\log(T_j/3)\le\sigma^{(j)}\le\log(3U_j)$.  These intervals are disjoint, as
$T_{j+1}>9U_j$, so the $\sigma^{(j)}$ are distinct.  For every $a$,
$\log r_a<\log(T_a/3)\le\sigma^{(a)}$ (for $a=1$ because $T_1>3$), and for
$a<S$ also $\sigma^{(a)}\le\log(3U_a)<\log r_{a+1}$.  Let
$\sigma^{(a)}=\sigma_q$ in the numbering of Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}.  By the
proof of \eqref{eq:centralabove}, a central index at a
radius below $e^{\sigma_q}$ is at most the lower vertex $v_q$ of the edge of
slope $-\sigma_q$; symmetrically, for $i<v_{q-1}$ the same edge gives
$\log|f_i|+ip<\log|f_{v_{q-1}}|+v_{q-1}p$ when $p>\sigma_q$, so a central
index at a radius above $e^{\sigma_q}$ is at least the upper vertex
$v_{q-1}>v_q$.  Hence $y_a\le v_q<v_{q-1}\le y_{a+1}$ for $a<S$, and
$y_S\le v_q<v_0=D$ for $a=S$.

\emph{Counts.}  Let $a\ge2$.  The $S-a+1$ distinct tropical roots
$\sigma^{(a)},\ldots,\sigma^{(S)}$ exceed $\log r_a$, so
$D-y_a\ge S-a+1$ by \eqref{eq:centralabove}.  Let $n=\min(n_a,\kappa_a)$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount} with $A$ any $n$ of the zeros in $A_{\ge a}$, of
modulus at least $T_a\ge3nr_a$, gives $D-y_a\ge n$.  So $D-y_a\ge k_a$ for every $a\ge2$.

\emph{Values.}  Every $r_a\ge1$ (for $a\ge2$, $r_a>3U_{a-1}\ge3T_1>9$).
Lemma~\ref{lem:central} with $A=A_{\ge a}$ gives
$|f_{y_a}|>\frac{|f_D|}2r_a^{D-y_a-n_a}\prod_{A_{\ge a}}\max(r_a,|\beta|/2)$.
For $a=1$ the power of $r_1=1$ is $1$; for $a\ge2$ it is at least
$r_a^{k_a-n_a}$, since $D-y_a\ge k_a$; and $\max(r_a,|\beta|/2)\ge|\beta|/2$.
This is $|f_{y_a}|>B_a$.

The positions $y_1,\ldots,y_k$ are $k$ distinct nonleading positions with
magnitudes above $\min_{a\le k}B_a$, which bounds $b_k(F)$.  For the mass,
$\Lambda(F)\ge\log^+|f_D|+\sum_a\log^+|f_{y_a}|\ge\log|f_D|+\sum_a\log B_a$,
and $\sum_a\sum_{\beta\in A_{\ge a}}=\sum_ss\sum_{\beta\in A_s}$; the term
$a=1$ has no power of $r_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Annuli]\label{cor:annuli}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If moreover $T_a>9n_aU_{a-1}$ for $2\le a\le S$, then
$b_k(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2$ for $1\le k\le S$,
and, if $|f_D|\ge1$,
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (S+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{s=1}^Ss\sum_{\beta\in A_s}\log\frac{|\beta|}2-S\log2 .
\]
\item Without the extra hypothesis, if $|f_D|\ge1$,
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (S+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{\beta\in A_{\ge1}}\log\frac{|\beta|}2
  +\sum_{a=2}^S\sum_{\beta\in A_{\ge a}}\log\frac{|\beta|}{6U_{a-1}}-S\log2 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
1.  Choose $r_a$ with $3U_{a-1}<r_a<T_a/(3n_a)$.  Then $r_a<T_a/3$ and
$\kappa_a\ge n_a$, so $k_a=n_a$ and
$B_a=\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{A_{\ge a}}\frac{|\beta|}2$.  Every factor
$|\beta|/2$ exceeds $1$, so $B_a$ decreases with $a$ and
$\min_{a\le k}B_a=B_k$.

2.  As $k_a\ge0$, $(n_a-k_a)\log r_a\le n_a\log r_a$, and
$\sum_{A_{\ge a}}\log\frac{|\beta|}2-n_a\log r_a=\sum_{A_{\ge a}}\log\frac{|\beta|}{2r_a}$.
The bound holds for every admissible $r_a$; let $r_a\downarrow3U_{a-1}$.
\end{proof}

Item~1 charges a root of $A_s$ once for each annulus at or below it, $s$
times in all, at the price of a separation that grows linearly with the
number of roots above; by the remark after Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}, a
central index at $r_a$ can have fewer than $n_a$ positions above it when
$T_a/r_a<n_a$, and the rows themselves fail at $T_a=n_aU_{a-1}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}); for $S=2$ they hold at
$T_2>(n_2+1)U_1$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}), and even at
$T_2>n_2(1+3\cdot2^{-n_2})U_1$ once $n_2\ge4$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:twofixed}), and for every $S$ at
$T_a>\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})U_{a-1}$ (Corollaries~\ref{cor:fewabove}
and~\ref{cor:rowsall}), with $n_a+1$ in place of $n_a+9$ where $n_a\ge30$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsplusone}).  Item~2 needs only the factor $9$, but past the first
annulus it charges ratios $|\beta|/U_{a-1}$ instead of moduli.  With one root
per annulus, $k_a=n_a=S-a+1$ at the separation $9$ of the theorem, which then
gives Corollary~\ref{cor:separated}, item~1, when $\rho_1>3$ (the corollary
allows $\rho_1=3$, the theorem needs $T_1>3$), with $\rho_j/2$ in place of
$\rho_j/3$ and an extra factor $\frac12$ in each row.  For the mass alone a
fixed separation suffices: where one central index falls short, two of them
share the charge.

\begin{theorem}[Annuli at a fixed separation]\label{thm:fixedgap}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$ with $|f_D|\ge1$, let
$S\ge1$, and let $A_1,\ldots,A_S$ be nonempty sub-multisets of the zeros of
$F$, counted with multiplicity, the moduli in $A_s$ lying in $[T_s,U_s]$,
where
\[
  T_1>3,\qquad T_a>81\,U_{a-1}\quad(2\le a\le S).
\]
Then
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ (S+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{s=1}^Ss\sum_{\beta\in A_s}\log\frac{|\beta|}2-S\log2 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} and put
$Z_a=\sum_{\beta\in A_{\ge a}}\log(|\beta|/2)$, so that
$\sum_aZ_a=\sum_ss\sum_{\beta\in A_s}\log(|\beta|/2)$ and
$Z_a\ge n_a\log(T_a/2)$.  The Order step of that proof uses only
$T_1>3$ and $T_{a}>9U_{a-1}$; it gives tropical roots
$\sigma^{(1)}<\cdots<\sigma^{(S)}$ with
$\log(T_a/3)\le\sigma^{(a)}\le\log(3U_a)$, and shows that a central index at
a radius below $e^{\sigma^{(a)}}$ lies below every central index at a radius
above it.  For $2\le a\le S$ choose
$u_a^-<u_a^+$, neither a tropical root, with
\[
  \log(3U_{a-1})<u_a^-,\qquad u_a^-+2\log3<u_a^+\le\log(T_a/3);
\]
this is possible as $T_a/3>9\cdot3U_{a-1}$.  In particular
$e^{u_a^-}<T_a/27$.  Let $Y_a$ be the set of the
vertices active at $u_a^-$ and at $u_a^+$, and let $y_1$ be a central index
at $1$.  As $\sigma^{(a-1)}<u_a^-<u_a^+<\sigma^{(a)}$ and $0<\sigma^{(1)}$,
$y_1$ lies below every position in $Y_2$, every
position in $Y_a$ below every position in $Y_{a+1}$, and every position in
$Y_S$ below $D$ ($y_1$ below $D$ if $S=1$).  So the positions in $\{y_1\}\cup Y_2\cup\cdots\cup Y_S$
are distinct and nonleading.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:central} at $r=1$ with $A=A_{\ge1}$,
$\log|f_{y_1}|>\log(|f_D|/2)+Z_1$.  Let $a\ge2$ and let $y\in Y_a$ be active
at $u\in\{u_a^-,u_a^+\}$, so $9<e^u\le T_a/3$.  Every $\beta\in A_{\ge a}$ has
$|\beta|/2>e^u$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:central} with $A=A_{\ge a}$ gives
$\log|f_y|>\log(|f_D|/2)+Z_a-\delta u$, where $\delta=n_a-D+y$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount} with $A=A_{\ge a}$ and $T=T_a$ gives
$\delta\le0.82\,n_a$ at $u_a^+$, where $T_a\ge3e^u$, and $\delta\le0.13\,n_a$
at $u_a^-$, where $T_a\ge27e^u$.

If $Y_a=\{y\}$, no tropical root lies in $[u_a^-,u_a^+]$ (one would separate
the two vertices, as in the Order step), so the midpoint $u$
of this interval is farther than $\log3$ from every tropical root, and $y$ is
the vertex active at $u$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~1, $F$ has
exactly $D-y$ zeros in $|z|>e^u$; the $n_a$ zeros of $A_{\ge a}$ are among
them, so $D-y\ge n_a$ and $\log|f_y|>\log(|f_D|/2)+Z_a$.  Otherwise $Y_a$
has two positions, active at $u_a^+\le\log(T_a/3)$ and at
$u_a^-<\log(T_a/27)$, and their logarithms add up to more than
\[
  2\log\frac{|f_D|}2+2Z_a-n_a\Bigl(0.82\log\frac{T_a}3+0.13\log\frac{T_a}{27}\Bigr)
  \ \ge\ 2\log\frac{|f_D|}2+Z_a+n_a\bigl(0.05\log T_a+1.21\log3-\log2\bigr),
\]
as $Z_a\ge n_a\log(T_a/2)$.  Since $T_a>81U_{a-1}\ge81T_1>3^5$, the last
bracket exceeds $1.46\log3-\log2>\log2$, and as $\log|f_D|\ge0$ the sum
exceeds $\log(|f_D|/2)+Z_a$.  In both cases
$\sum_{y\in Y_a}\log^+|f_y|\ge\log|f_D|+Z_a-\log2$, and
\[
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ \log^+|f_D|+\log^+|f_{y_1}|+\sum_{a=2}^S\sum_{y\in Y_a}\log^+|f_y|
  \ \ge\ (S+1)\log|f_D|+\sum_{a=1}^SZ_a-S\log2 .
\]
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} bounds the mass only: below the $a$-th annulus
the charge of the roots in the $a$-th and higher annuli may fall on two
coefficients rather than one, and the rows of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli},
item~1, fail at every fixed separation (Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}).
The factor $81$ cannot be lowered below $2+\frac1{18915}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gaptwo}), and for two annuli $\max(4,n_2+1)$
suffices (Corollary~\ref{cor:twofixed}), and $\frac52$ when $n_2\le2$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:twonear});
the least admissible factor is open.  The mass bound is sharp to leading order, the
coefficient $s$ of each $K_s\log T_s$ being best possible, as
$\min_sT_s\to\infty$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:annulisharp} has
$\Lambda=\sum_ssN_s\log t_s$ against the bound
$\sum_ssN_s\log(t_s/6)-S\log2$), and the cross terms $K_uK_s\log T_s$ of the
real bound fail.

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness for annuli]\label{prop:annulisharp}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $N_1,\ldots,N_S\ge1$ and let $2\le t_1<\cdots<t_S$ be integers with
$t_{a+1}>9t_a$.  Put $p_a=N_1+\cdots+N_a$ ($p_0=0$), $D=p_S$ and
\[
  F=\sum_{a=0}^Sc_ax^{p_a},\qquad c_a=\prod_{s>a}t_s^{N_s}.
\]
Then $F\in\mathbb Z[x]$ is monic, $F$ has exactly $N_s$ zeros, counted with
multiplicity, in $t_s/3\le|z|\le3t_s$ for each $s$ and no other zeros, and
\[
  \Lambda(F)=\sum_{s=1}^SsN_s\log t_s .
\]
If moreover $t_1>9$ and $t_a>729\,t_{a-1}$, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} applies to $F$ with $A_s$ the zeros of modulus in
$[t_s/3,3t_s]$, and gives $\Lambda(F)\ge\sum_ssN_s\log(t_s/6)-S\log2$.
\item Let $0<\delta<\pi/2$, let $M_1\ne M_2$ be odd integers at least $3$,
and let $1<t_1<t_2$.  The monic real polynomial
$(x^{2M_1}+t_1^{2M_1})(x^{2M_2}+t_2^{2M_2})$ is divisible, for $s=1,2$, by
$K_s=2\lfloor\delta M_s/\pi\rfloor+1$ conjugate pairs of modulus $t_s$
within $\delta$ of the imaginary axis, and
\[
  \Lambda=4M_1\log t_1+4M_2\log t_2\ \le\
  \frac{2\pi}\delta\bigl((K_1+1)\log t_1+(K_2+1)\log t_2\bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for no $c>0$ does $\Lambda(F)\ge c\,K_1K_2\log T_2$ hold for
all monic multiples $F$ of $K_1$ roots with moduli in $[T_1,9T_1]$ and $K_2$
roots with moduli in $[T_2,9T_2]$, however large $T_2/T_1$ is, nor with $K_s$
counting conjugate pairs within a fixed angle of the imaginary axis, as in
item~2.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
1.  $F$ has the $S+1$ nonzero coefficients $c_a\in\mathbb Z$ at the positions
$p_a$, and $c_S=1$.  The slope from $(p_{a-1},\log c_{a-1})$ to
$(p_a,\log c_a)$ is $-\log t_a$, which decreases strictly with $a$, so these
points are the vertices of the least concave majorant, and the tropical roots
are the $\log t_a$, the edge of $\log t_a$ joining $p_{a-1}$ to $p_a$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~2, every zero lies in one of the closed
annuli $t_s/3\le|z|\le3t_s$, which are disjoint as $t_{s+1}>9t_s$.  For $0\le a\le S$
choose $u_a$ with $3t_a<e^{u_a}<t_{a+1}/3$ (with $t_0=0$ and $t_{S+1}=\infty$).
It is farther than $\log3$ from every tropical root, and the vertex active at
$u_a$ is $p_a$; by Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~1, $F$ has exactly
$p_a$ zeros in $|z|<e^{u_a}$, that is, in the first $a$ annuli.  So the
$a$-th annulus holds $p_a-p_{a-1}=N_a$ zeros.  Every $c_a\ge1$, so
$\Lambda(F)=\sum_{a<S}\log c_a=\sum_{a<S}\sum_{s>a}N_s\log t_s
=\sum_ssN_s\log t_s$.

For the last claim take $T_s=t_s/3$ and $U_s=3t_s$: then $T_1>3$,
$T_a>81U_{a-1}$, $|A_s|=N_s$, $f_D=1$, and $|\beta|/2\ge t_s/6$ on $A_s$.

2.  By the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:noangle}, item~1, each factor
$x^{2M_s}+t_s^{2M_s}$ has $K_s$ conjugate pairs of modulus $t_s$ within
$\delta$ of the imaginary axis, and $2M_s\le\pi(K_s+1)/\delta$.  The product
is $x^{2M_1+2M_2}+t_2^{2M_2}x^{2M_1}+t_1^{2M_1}x^{2M_2}+t_1^{2M_1}t_2^{2M_2}$,
four distinct positions as $M_1\ne M_2$, which gives $\Lambda$.

For the final sentence, item~1 with $S=2$, $N_1=N_2=N$, $t_1\ge9$ and $T_s=t_s/3$ (so $T_s\ge3$)
has $K_s=N$ zeros with moduli in $[T_s,9T_s]$ and
$\Lambda(F)=N\log t_1+2N\log t_2\le3N\log(3T_2)\le6N\log T_2$, which is below
$cN^2\log T_2$ once $N>6/c$.  Item~2 with $T_s=t_s$ has
$\Lambda\le\frac{2\pi}\delta(K_1+K_2+2)\log T_2$, below $cK_1K_2\log T_2$
once $M_1$ and $M_2$, hence $K_1$ and $K_2$, are large.
\end{proof}

So at complex roots in separated annuli the sharp worst-case analogue of
$\sum_ii\log r_i$ is $\sum_ssK_s\log T_s$: the cross term between annuli $u<s$ is $K_s\log T_s$,
linear in the number of roots above and independent of the number below.  For
the mass it is proved at the fixed separation $81$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap}), and with ratios in place of moduli at the
separation $9$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~2); the rows of item~1 are
proved at the separation $9n_a$, at $3\rho_{n_a}<7n_a$ by
Corollary~\ref{cor:fewabove}, at $n_a+9$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsall}, and
at $n_a+1$ where $n_a\ge30$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsplusone}, and the
factor $n_a$ cannot be dropped; for two annuli $n_2+1$ suffices
(Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}).

\begin{proposition}[Rows need the factor $n_a$]\label{prop:rowsneedn}
Let $K\ge10$ and $q\ge4^K$ be integers and $F=(x-q)(x+Kq)^K$.  Then
Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} applies to the monic $F\in\mathbb Z[x]$ with
$S=2$, $A_1=\{q\}$ and $A_2$ the $K$-fold zero $-Kq$, so that
$T_2=U_2=Kq=n_2U_1$, and
\[
  f_1=0,\qquad b_2(F)\ <\ 2^K(Kq)^{K-1}\ \le\ \frac12\prod_{\beta\in A_2}\frac{|\beta|}2 .
\]
So the row $k=2$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, fails at $T_2=n_2U_1$.
Moreover $b_2(F)<2^K(Kq)^{-1}\prod_{\beta\in A_2}|\beta|$, so for no $g$ and
$\varepsilon>0$ does $b_2(F)\ge\varepsilon|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A_2}|\beta|$
hold for all $F$ with $T_1>3$ and $T_2>gU_1$: take $K>g$ and $q\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $h_i=\binom Ki(Kq)^{K-i}$ be the coefficients of $(x+Kq)^K$.  Then
$f_0=-qh_0$, $f_{K+1}=1$ and, for $1\le i\le K$,
\[
  f_i=h_{i-1}-qh_i=q(Kq)^{K-i}\Bigl(K\binom K{i-1}-\binom Ki\Bigr).
\]
The bracket vanishes at $i=1$, and for $2\le i\le K$ it lies in
$[0,K\binom K{i-1})$, as $\binom Ki/\binom K{i-1}=(K-i+1)/i\le K$; since
$\binom K{i-1}<2^K$ and $(Kq)^{K-i}\le(Kq)^{K-2}$,
$0\le f_i<2^KKq(Kq)^{K-2}=2^K(Kq)^{K-1}$.  Every nonleading position other
than $0$ is one of these, so $b_2(F)<2^K(Kq)^{K-1}$, and
$2^K(Kq)^{K-1}\le(Kq)^K/2^{K+1}$ as $Kq\ge K4^K\ge2^{2K+1}$.  The
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} hold: $T_1=q>3$ and $T_2=Kq>9U_1$.
\end{proof}

Here $\sum_{\beta\in A_2}1/\beta=-1/q$, which is why $f_1$ vanishes: the
zeros above cancel the one below at the position next to the constant term,
the full charge of $A_2$ below the second annulus is left on $f_0$ alone,
and every other nonleading coefficient carries fewer than $K$ zeros of
$A_2$.  The cancellation needs $|\sum_{\beta\in A_2}1/\beta|=1/q$, hence
$T_2\le n_2U_1$.  So the separation the rows of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli},
item~1, need grows linearly with $n_a$: it is at most
$\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})$, where $3\rho_{n_a}<7n_a$
(Corollaries~\ref{cor:fewabove} and~\ref{cor:rowsall}), and $n_a+1$ where
$n_a\ge30$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsplusone}), and not at most $n_a$.

For two annuli this cancellation is the only obstruction, and the factor
$7n_2$ drops to $n_2+1$.  Dividing out the zeros above leaves a quotient
whose coefficients below the upper zeros are truncations of
$\prod_\beta(1-x/\beta)^{-1}$, and at a lower zero such a truncation is
bounded away from $0$.  Write $h_k$ for the complete homogeneous symmetric
polynomial of degree $k$, so that
$\prod_{i=1}^n(1-x_iz)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}h_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n)z^k$.

\begin{lemma}[Truncations]\label{lem:trunc}
Let $n\ge1$, $0\le\rho\le1/n$, and $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbb C$ with
$|x_i|\le\rho$.  Then for every $M\ge0$
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{k=0}^Mh_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\Bigr|\ \ge\ 1-n\rho .
\]
Equality holds at $M=1$, $x_1=\cdots=x_n=-\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $M=0$ the sum is $1$.  For $n=1$ and $M\ge1$ it is
$(1-x_1^{M+1})/(1-x_1)$ (or $M+1$ if $x_1=1$), of modulus at least
$(1-\rho^{M+1})/(1+\rho)\ge(1-\rho^2)/(1+\rho)=1-\rho$.  Let $n\ge2$, and let
$u$ be uniform on the simplex $u_i\ge0$, $\sum_iu_i=1$.  Its moments are
$\mathbb E\prod_iu_i^{a_i}=(n-1)!\prod_ia_i!/(n-1+\sum_ia_i)!$: for independent
standard exponential $g_1,\ldots,g_n$, the substitution $g=su$ has
$dg=s^{n-1}\,ds\,du$, so $s=\sum_ig_i$, with density $s^{n-1}e^{-s}/(n-1)!$,
and $u=g/s$, uniform, are independent, and
$\prod_ia_i!=\mathbb E\prod_ig_i^{a_i}=\mathbb E\,s^{\sum_ia_i}\,\mathbb E\prod_iu_i^{a_i}$.  So expanding
$(u\cdot x)^k$ by the multinomial theorem gives
$\mathbb E(u\cdot x)^k=h_k(x)/\binom{n+k-1}k$, and
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^Mh_k(x)=\mathbb E\,\tau_M(u\cdot x),\qquad
  \tau_M(y)=\sum_{k=0}^M\binom{n+k-1}ky^k ,
\]
with $|u\cdot x|\le\rho$.  So it suffices that $\operatorname{Re}\tau_M(y)\ge1-n|y|$
for $|y|\le1/n$; then the real part of the sum is at least $1-n\rho$.  Let
$y=\rho'e^{i\theta}$ with $0<\rho'\le1/n$ and $\theta\notin2\pi\mathbb Z$
(for $y\ge0$ every term is nonnegative), and put
$c_k=\binom{n+k-1}k\rho'^k$, so $c_0=1$, $c_1=n\rho'$ and
$c_{k+1}=a_k\rho'c_k$ with $a_k=(n+k)/(k+1)$.  Let
$D_k=\sum_{i=0}^k\cos i\theta$ and $E_k=\sum_{j=1}^kD_j$; by Fej\'er's
identity $\sum_{j=0}^kD_j=\frac{k+1}2+\Phi_k$ with
$\Phi_k=\sin^2\frac{(k+1)\theta}2\big/\bigl(2\sin^2\frac\theta2\bigr)\ge0$
(telescoping $2\sin\frac\theta2\cos i\theta=\sin(i+\frac12)\theta-\sin(i-\frac12)\theta$
gives $D_j=\frac12+\sin(j+\frac12)\theta/(2\sin\frac\theta2)$, and
telescoping $2\sin\frac\theta2\sin(j+\frac12)\theta=\cos j\theta-\cos(j+1)\theta$
sums these), so
$E_k=\frac{k-1}2+\Phi_k\ge0$ for $k\ge1$.  With $e_k=c_k-c_{k+1}$ for $k<M$,
$e_M=c_M$ and $e_{M+1}=0$, summation by parts twice gives
\[
  \operatorname{Re}\tau_M(y)=\sum_{k=0}^Mc_k\cos k\theta
  =1-c_1+\sum_{k=1}^Me_kD_k=1-c_1+\sum_{k=1}^M(e_k-e_{k+1})E_k .
\]
For $M=1$ the last sum is $c_1E_1\ge0$.  Let $M\ge2$.  For $1\le k\le M-2$,
$e_k-e_{k+1}=c_k(1-2a_k\rho'+a_ka_{k+1}\rho'^2)$; the quadratic decreases on
$[0,1/n]$, as its vertex $1/a_{k+1}$ is at least $1/n$, and
$n^2(k+1)(k+2)$ times its value at $1/n$ is $(n-1)\bigl((n-1)k(k+1)-n\bigr)\ge0$.
The terms $k=M-1,M$ add up to $c_{M-1}\bigl((1-2r)E_{M-1}+rE_M\bigr)$ with
$r=c_M/c_{M-1}=(n+M-1)\rho'/M\le(M+1)/(2M)$.  If $r\le\frac12$ this is
nonnegative; otherwise $2-1/r\le2/(M+1)$ and it is at least
$c_{M-1}r\bigl(E_M-2E_{M-1}/(M+1)\bigr)$.  Finally $(M+1)E_M\ge2E_{M-1}$: for
$M=2$ the difference is $1+4\cos\theta+6\cos^2\theta>0$, and for $M\ge3$,
$|\sin\frac{M\theta}2|\le|\sin\frac{(M+1)\theta}2|+|\sin\frac\theta2|$ gives
$\Phi_{M-1}\le2\Phi_M+1$, so the difference is at least
$\frac{M^2-2M-1}2+(M-3)\Phi_M>0$.  Hence $\operatorname{Re}\tau_M(y)\ge1-c_1=1-n|y|$.
Equality: $1+h_1=1-n\rho$ at $x_i=-\rho$.
\end{proof}

For $n=1$ the real part alone does not suffice: $\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k\le M}y^k$
can be negative for $|y|<1$.

\begin{theorem}[The second row at the separation $n$]\label{thm:rowstwo}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, let $A$ be a sub-multiset of
the zeros of $F$, counted with multiplicity, with $n=|A|\ge1$ and
$|\beta|\ge T$ for $\beta\in A$, and let $\alpha$ be a zero of $F$ with
$1<|\alpha|<T/n$.  Put $\lambda=1-n|\alpha|/T$, and $\gamma=1$ if $n=1$,
$\gamma=(1-|\alpha|/T)^n$ if $n\ge2$.  Then
\[
  b_2(F)\ \ge\ \frac{\lambda\gamma}2\Bigl(1-\frac1{|\alpha|}\Bigr)|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta| ,
\]
and $\gamma\ge\max(\lambda,\frac14)$, so $b_2(F)$ is at least
$\frac\lambda8(1-\frac1{|\alpha|})|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$ and at least
$\frac{\lambda^2}2(1-\frac1{|\alpha|})|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Divide $F$ by $f_D$, so $f_D=1$.  Put $a=|\alpha|$,
$\Pi=\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$ and
$\varepsilon=\frac{\lambda\gamma}2(1-\frac1a)$, and suppose
$b_2(F)<\varepsilon\Pi$.  Then some position $j<D$ has $|f_l|<\varepsilon\Pi$
for every $l<D$ other than $j$.  For $|z|>1$ the polynomial
$E=F-f_jx^j-x^D$ has $|E(z)|<\varepsilon\Pi|z|^D/(|z|-1)$.

\emph{The case $n=1$.}  Let $A=\{\beta\}$, $\rho=a/|\beta|$ and
$z=\alpha/\beta$, so $\Pi=|\beta|>a$ and $1-\rho\ge\lambda$.  Multiplying
$f_j\beta^j=-\beta^D-E(\beta)$ by $z^j$ and subtracting
$f_j\alpha^j=-\alpha^D-E(\alpha)$ gives
$\beta^Dz^j-\alpha^D=E(\alpha)-z^jE(\beta)$.  The left side has modulus
$|\beta|^D\rho^j|1-z^{D-j}|\ge|\beta|^D\rho^j\lambda$; dividing by
$|\beta|^D\rho^j$, and as $\rho^{D-j}\Pi\le\rho|\beta|=a$,
\[
  \lambda\ <\ \varepsilon\Pi\Bigl(\frac{\rho^{D-j}}{a-1}+\frac1{|\beta|-1}\Bigr)
  \ \le\ \varepsilon\Bigl(\frac a{a-1}+\frac{|\beta|}{|\beta|-1}\Bigr)
  \ <\ \frac{2\varepsilon a}{a-1}\ =\ \lambda ,
\]
a contradiction.

\emph{The case $n\ge2$.}  Let $H=\prod_{\beta\in A}(x-\beta)$ and $P=F/H$,
monic of degree $d=D-n<D$; as $|\alpha|<T$, $P(\alpha)=0$.  For $|x|<T$,
$H(0)/H(x)=\prod_\beta(1-x/\beta)^{-1}=\sum_kw_kx^k$ with
$w_k=h_k(1/\beta)$, $|w_k|\le\binom{n+k-1}kT^{-k}$.  Put
$S_m(x)=\sum_{k\le m}w_kx^k$.  As $H(0)P=F\cdot H(0)/H$ and $P$ has degree
$d$, comparing coefficients up to $x^d$ gives
\[
  H(0)P(x)=\sum_{l=0}^df_lx^lS_{d-l}(x).
\]
For $|x|\le a$ and every $m$,
$|S_m(x)|\le\sum_k\binom{n+k-1}k(a/T)^k=(1-a/T)^{-n}=1/\gamma$, and
$\sum_k|w_k|\le1/\gamma$ as $a>1$; and Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc} with
$x_i=\alpha/\beta_i$, $\rho=a/T$, gives $|S_m(\alpha)|\ge\lambda$.
The coefficient of $x^d$ is $H(0)=\sum_{l\le d}f_lw_{d-l}$, and
$\varepsilon/\gamma\le\frac12$.  If $j>d$, this gives
$\Pi<\varepsilon\Pi/\gamma<\Pi$; so $j\le d$, and with $M=d-j$,
$|f_j||w_M|\ge\Pi(1-\varepsilon/\gamma)$, so
$|f_j|\ge\Pi(1-\varepsilon/\gamma)T^M/\binom{n+M-1}M$.  At $x=\alpha$,
$H(0)P(\alpha)=0$ gives
\[
  |f_j|a^j\lambda\ \le\ |f_j\alpha^jS_M(\alpha)|\ \le\ \sum_{l\le d,\,l\ne j}|f_l|a^l|S_{d-l}(\alpha)|
  \ <\ \frac{\varepsilon\Pi}\gamma\cdot\frac{a^{d+1}}{a-1} .
\]
Together, as $\binom{n+M-1}M\le n^M$ and $na/T<1$,
\[
  \frac\lambda2\ \le\ \lambda\Bigl(1-\frac\varepsilon\gamma\Bigr)
  \ <\ \frac\varepsilon\gamma\cdot\frac a{a-1}\binom{n+M-1}M\Bigl(\frac aT\Bigr)^M
  \ \le\ \frac\varepsilon\gamma\cdot\frac a{a-1}=\frac\lambda2 ,
\]
a contradiction.  Finally, for $n\ge2$, $(1-a/T)^n\ge1-na/T=\lambda$ by
Bernoulli's inequality, and $(1-a/T)^n>(1-\frac1n)^n\ge\frac14$ as $a/T<1/n$.
\end{proof}

The order $\lambda$ is sharp: $(x-q)(x+t)^K$ with $t=Kq/(1-\lambda)$ has
$f_1=\lambda t^K$, so $b_2(F)=\lambda\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$ once $q$ is
large, and at $\lambda=0$ this is Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}.  For
$n=1$ the pointwise bound $|S_m(\alpha)|\le1/\gamma$ gives only $\lambda^2$,
as $|S_m(\alpha)|$ can be close to $1/\lambda$; the proof uses both zeros
instead.  Summing the truncations against the powers of $|\alpha|$ first
removes the factor $\gamma$, and the constant becomes $1$ as $|\alpha|$
grows.

\begin{proposition}[The second row at a large lower zero]\label{prop:rowstwolarge}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo}, put $a=|\alpha|$,
$\Pi=\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$ and $\Gamma=(1-1/T)^{-n}$.  Then
$\Gamma\le a/(a-1+\lambda)$ and
\[
  b_2(F)\ \ge\ \min\Bigl(\frac{\lambda(a-1)}{\Gamma(a-\lambda)},\
  \frac{a-1}{1+(2a-1)(\Gamma-1)}\Bigr)|f_D|\,\Pi .
\]
In particular $b_2(F)\ge\lambda(1-\frac1a)^2|f_D|\Pi$ if $a\ge2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Divide $F$ by $f_D$, so $f_D=1$; let $\varepsilon$ be the minimum and
suppose $b_2(F)<\varepsilon\Pi$, so that some $j<D$ has $|f_l|<\varepsilon\Pi$ for every
$l<D$ other than $j$.  Let $H$, $P$, $d=D-n$, $w_m$ and $S_m$ be as in the
case $n\ge2$ of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo}, which needs only
$n\ge1$, and $W_m=\binom{n+m-1}mT^{-m}$, so $|w_m|\le W_m$,
$\sum_mW_m=\Gamma$ and $|S_m(\alpha)|\le\sum_{i\le m}W_ia^i$.  Hence
\[
  \sum_{l\le d}a^l|S_{d-l}(\alpha)|\ \le\ \sum_iW_i\sum_{l\le d-i}a^{l+i}
  \ <\ \frac{\Gamma a^{d+1}}{a-1},
\]
and, as $S_0=1$, $\sum_{l<d}a^l|S_{d-l}(\alpha)|<(1+a(\Gamma-1))a^d/(a-1)$.
As $\varepsilon\Gamma\le\lambda(a-1)/(a-\lambda)<1$, the coefficient
$H(0)=\sum_{l\le d}f_lw_{d-l}$ of $x^d$ forces $j\le d$; put $M=d-j$.  If
$M\ge1$, then $|f_j|W_M>\Pi(1-\varepsilon\Gamma)$, and at $x=\alpha$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}, $\lambda|f_j|a^j\le|f_j\alpha^jS_M(\alpha)|<\varepsilon\Pi\Gamma
a^{d+1}/(a-1)$; as $W_Ma^M\le(na/T)^M\le1-\lambda$, together
$\lambda(1-\varepsilon\Gamma)<\varepsilon\Gamma(1-\lambda)a/(a-1)$, that is,
$\varepsilon>\lambda(a-1)/(\Gamma(a-\lambda))$.  If $M=0$, then
$|f_d|>\Pi(1-\varepsilon(\Gamma-1))$ and $|f_d|a^d<\varepsilon\Pi(1+a(\Gamma-1))a^d/(a-1)$,
so $\varepsilon>(a-1)/(1+(2a-1)(\Gamma-1))$.  Both contradict the choice of
$\varepsilon$.

By Bernoulli's inequality $\Gamma^{-1}\ge1-n/T=1-(1-\lambda)/a$.  Let
$a\ge2$.  Then the first term of the minimum is at least
$\lambda(a-1)(a-1+\lambda)/(a(a-\lambda))\ge\lambda(a-1)^2/a^2$, as
$2a\lambda\ge\lambda$, and the second is at least
$(a-1)(a-1+\lambda)/(3a-2+2\lambda-2a\lambda)$, which is at least the first
bound as
$a(a-\lambda)-\lambda(3a-2+2\lambda-2a\lambda)=a(a-2)+2a(1-\lambda)^2+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\ge0$.
\end{proof}

So the order $\lambda$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo} holds with the constant
$1-O(1/|\alpha|)$, while $(x-q)(x+t)^K$ above has $b_2(F)=\lambda|f_D|\Pi$
at $|\alpha|=q$: the best constant $c$ in
$b_2(F)\ge c\lambda(1-\frac1{|\alpha|})|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$ over
$|\alpha|\ge a_0$ lies in $[1-\frac1{a_0},1]$ for $a_0\ge2$, and tends to
$1$; over all $|\alpha|>1$ it lies in $[\frac18,1]$.

\begin{corollary}[Rows for two annuli]\label{cor:rowstwo}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} with $S\ge2$, let $C>1$.  If
$T_2\ge Cn_2U_1$, then $b_2(F)\ge\frac13(1-\frac1C)^2|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge2}}|\beta|$
and $b_2(F)\ge\frac1{12}(1-\frac1C)|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge2}}|\beta|$.  If
$T_2>(n_2+1)U_1$, then
$b_2(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge2}}\frac{|\beta|}2$; so for $S=2$
the rows and the mass bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, hold with
$T_2>(n_2+1)U_1$ in place of $T_2>9n_2U_1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\alpha\in A_1$, so $3<T_1\le|\alpha|\le U_1$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo} with $A=A_{\ge2}$, $n=n_2$ and $T=T_2$ (every $T_a$
with $a\ge2$ is at least $T_2$).  Then $1-1/|\alpha|>\frac23$ and
$\lambda\ge1-n_2U_1/T_2$.  If $T_2\ge Cn_2U_1$, $\lambda\ge1-1/C$, and
the theorem gives $\frac23\cdot\frac{\lambda^2}2$ and $\frac23\cdot\frac\lambda8$.  If
$T_2>(n_2+1)U_1$, then, as also $T_2>9U_1$, $\lambda>\lambda_0=1-n_2/\max(9,n_2+1)$,
and $b_2(F)>\frac13\lambda_0^2|f_D|\prod_{A_{\ge2}}|\beta|$; it remains that
$\lambda_0^2\ge3\cdot2^{-n_2-1}$.  For $n_2\le8$, $\lambda_0=(9-n_2)/9$ and
$(9-n_2)^22^{n_2+1}\ge256>243$; for $n_2\ge8$, $\lambda_0=1/(n_2+1)$ and
$2^{n_2+1}\ge3(n_2+1)^2$, true at $8$ and preserved as $n_2$ grows.  For
$S=2$ the row $k=1$ is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}, whose bound $B_1$
needs no condition on $T_2$, and the two rows give the mass as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}: $\Lambda(F)\ge\log^+|f_D|+\log^+b_1(F)+\log^+b_2(F)$.
\end{proof}

So for two annuli the rows need a separation between $n_2U_1$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}, with $n_2\ge10$) and $(n_2+1)U_1$, and
in the form $b_2(F)\ge\varepsilon|f_D|\prod_{A_{\ge2}}|\beta|$ the least
constant in a separation $Cn_2U_1$ is $C=1$: every $C>1$ gives
$\varepsilon=\frac13(1-\frac1C)^2$, independent of $n_2$, and $C=1$ gives no
$\varepsilon>0$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:rowstwolarge}, as $|\alpha|>3$,
also $\varepsilon=\frac49(1-\frac1C)$; and $T_2>(n_2+c)U_1$ with $c>0$ gives
$\lambda>c/(n_2+c)$ and
$b_2(F)>\frac49\cdot\frac c{n_2+c}|f_D|\prod_{A_{\ge2}}|\beta|$, which is the row
once $\frac89c\,2^{n_2}\ge n_2+c$, so for the row $2$ every additive
constant $c>0$ suffices once $n_2$ is large, for every $S$.  With $S=2$ and $n_2\le8$ this also gives the mass bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} at the separation $9$; Corollary~\ref{cor:twofixed}
below lowers these to $\max(4,n_2+1)$, and to $n_2(1+3\cdot2^{-n_2})$ for
$n_2\ge4$, against $n_2/(1-2^{-n_2-1})$ from below.

For $S\ge3$ the rows $k\ge3$ need several lower zeros at once.  The $k-1$
positions below the $(k-1)$-st annulus come from central indices, which need
no factor $n$ there; only the last position needs a truncation.

\begin{lemma}[Positions below]\label{lem:rowsbelow}
Let $F$, $A_1,\ldots,A_S$, $K_s$ and $n_a$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}, let $2\le k\le S$ and $n=n_k$, and assume of the
moduli only $T_1>3$, $T_a>U_{a-1}$ for $2\le a\le S$ and $T_a>9U_{a-1}$ for
$2\le a<k$.  Let $1\le j\le k$, $r_1=1$, $3U_{a-1}<r_a$ for $2\le a\le j$
and $r_a<T_a/3$ for $a\le j$, $a<k$, and suppose $T_k>\rho_nr_j$, with
$\rho_n$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}.  Then central indices $y_a$ of $F$ at $r_a$ satisfy
$y_1<\cdots<y_j$ and $D-y_j\ge n$, and
\[
  |f_{y_a}|\ >\ \frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2\qquad(1\le a\le j).
\]
In particular, if $T_k>3\rho_{n_k}U_{k-1}$, for instance if
$T_k>7n_kU_{k-1}$, then
$b_k(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $a<j$, Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~2, gives a tropical root
$\sigma^{(a)}$ with $\log r_a<\log(T_a/3)\le\sigma^{(a)}\le\log(3U_a)<\log
r_{a+1}$, so $y_a<y_{a+1}$ as in the Order step of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}.  The zeros of $A_{\ge k}$ have modulus at least
$T_k>\rho_nr_j$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount} gives $D-y_j\ge n$, and
$D-y_a\ge n$ for every $a\le j$.  As $r_a\ge1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:central} with
$A=A_{\ge k}$ gives
$|f_{y_a}|>\frac{|f_D|}2r_a^{D-y_a-n}\prod_{A_{\ge k}}\max(r_a,|\beta|/2)$,
which is at least the claim.  If $T_k>3\rho_nU_{k-1}$, take $j=k$ and
$3U_{k-1}<r_k<T_k/\rho_n$; the $k$ positions $y_a$ bound $b_k(F)$, and
$3\rho_n<7n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}.
\end{proof}

So the row $k$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, needs the factor
$3\rho_{n_k}$ only at the $k$-th separation, and the factor $9$ below it.  Below it, a truncation at a zero of the
$(k-1)$-st annulus gives the last position as in Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo},
while the central index at a radius just above $3U_{k-2}$ bounds what the
lower positions contribute there.

\begin{theorem}[The row $k$ at the separation $n_k+O(1)$]\label{thm:rowsk}
With the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow}, let $k\ge3$, $n=n_k$,
$T=T_k$ and $\Pi=\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}|\beta|$, and let $\alpha\in
A_{k-1}$ with $|\alpha|<T/n$.  Put $\lambda=1-n|\alpha|/T$,
$\gamma=(1-|\alpha|/T)^n$, $\xi=3U_{k-2}/T_{k-1}<\frac13$ and
\[
  \varepsilon=\frac\gamma2\Bigl(1-\frac1{|\alpha|}\Bigr)
  \Bigl(\lambda(1-2\xi)-\frac{(1-\lambda)\xi}{1-\xi}\Bigr).
\]
Then $b_k(F)\ge\min(2^{-n-1},\varepsilon)|f_D|\Pi$, and if
$\varepsilon>2^{-n-1}$, then $b_k(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Divide $F$ by $f_D$, so $f_D=1$, and put $a=|\alpha|$.  For $\xi<x<\frac13$
let $\varepsilon(x)$ be $\varepsilon$ with $x$ in place of $\xi$; it is
continuous, and nothing is to be proved if $\varepsilon\le0$.  So it suffices
that $b_k(F)>\theta\Pi$, with $\theta=\min(2^{-n-1},\varepsilon(x))$, for
every such $x$ with $\varepsilon(x)>0$; if $\varepsilon>2^{-n-1}$, then
$\theta=2^{-n-1}$ for $x$ close to $\xi$.

Let $r=xT_{k-1}$, so $3U_{k-2}<r<T_{k-1}/3$, $r\le xa$, and $\rho_nr<3nr<na<T$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} with $j=k-1$ and $r_{k-1}=r$ gives positions
$y_1<\cdots<y_{k-1}=y$ with $D-y\ge n$ and $|f_{y_a}|>2^{-n-1}\Pi\ge\theta\Pi$.
Suppose that every other nonleading position $l$ has $|f_l|\le\theta\Pi$.
Let $H$, $P$, $d=D-n\ge y$, $w_m$ and $S_m$ be as in the case $n\ge2$ of the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo} (they need only $n\ge1$), $M=d-y$ and $W_m=\binom{n+m-1}mT^{-m}$.  Then
$|w_m|\le W_m$, $W_ma^m\le(na/T)^m=(1-\lambda)^m$ and $W_{m+1}\le(n/T)W_m$;
$|S_m(\alpha)|\le1/\gamma$ and $\sum_m|w_m|\le1/\gamma$; and
$|S_M(\alpha)|\ge\lambda$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}.  As $y$ is a central
index at $r$, every $l<y$ has $|f_l|a^l\le|f_y|a^yx^{y-l}$ and, as
$nr/T\le x(1-\lambda)\le x$, $|f_l|W_{d-l}\le|f_y|W_Mx^{y-l}$.  The positions
$y<l\le d$ are nonleading and none of the $y_a$.

The coefficient of $x^d$ is $H(0)=\sum_{l\le d}f_lw_{d-l}$, so
$\Pi\le|f_y|W_M\sum_{i\ge0}x^i+\theta\Pi/\gamma$, that is,
$|f_y|W_M\ge(1-x)(1-\theta/\gamma)\Pi$.  At $x=\alpha$, $H(0)P(\alpha)=0$
reads
\[
  S_M(\alpha)\sum_{l\le y}f_l\alpha^l+\sum_{l<y}f_l\alpha^l\bigl(S_{d-l}(\alpha)-S_M(\alpha)\bigr)
  +\sum_{y<l\le d}f_l\alpha^lS_{d-l}(\alpha)\ =\ 0 .
\]
Here $|\sum_{l\le y}f_l\alpha^l|\ge|f_y|a^y(1-\frac x{1-x})$, and
$|S_{d-l}(\alpha)-S_M(\alpha)|\le\sum_{m=M+1}^{M+y-l}W_ma^m\le(y-l)(1-\lambda)$,
so the middle sum is at most $|f_y|a^y(1-\lambda)x/(1-x)^2$; the last is
below $\theta\Pi\gamma^{-1}a^{d+1}/(a-1)$.  Hence
\[
  |f_y|a^y\mu\ <\ \frac{\theta\Pi}\gamma\cdot\frac{a^{d+1}}{a-1},\qquad
  \mu=\frac{\lambda(1-2x)}{1-x}-\frac{(1-\lambda)x}{(1-x)^2}
  =\frac{2\varepsilon(x)}{\gamma(1-\frac1a)(1-x)}\ >\ 0 .
\]
With the bound on $|f_y|W_M$ and $W_Ma^M\le1$ this gives
$(1-x)(1-\theta/\gamma)\mu<(\theta/\gamma)a/(a-1)$.  But $\theta\le\varepsilon(x)$
gives $(\theta/\gamma)a/(a-1)\le(1-x)\mu/2$ and $\theta/\gamma\le(1-x)\mu/2\le\frac12$,
as $(1-x)\mu\le\lambda$; so the left side is at least $(1-x)\mu/2$, a
contradiction.  So some nonleading position other than the $y_a$ has
$|f_l|>\theta\Pi$, and $b_k(F)>\theta\Pi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rows at the separation $\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})$]\label{cor:rowsall}
Let $F$, $A_1,\ldots,A_S$, $K_s$ and $n_a$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}, except that of the moduli only $T_1>3$ is
assumed, and let $\rho_n$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}.  If
$T_a>\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})U_{a-1}$ for $2\le a\le S$, then the rows and
the mass bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, hold.  Here
$\min(n+9,3\rho_n)$ is $6$ for $n=1$ and $n+9$ for $n\ge2$.  More precisely,
with the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} and $k\ge3$, the row
$b_k(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2$ holds if
$T_k>3\rho_{n_k}U_{k-1}$, or if $T_{k-1}>(n_{k-1}+9)U_{k-2}$ and
$T_k>(n_k+9)U_{k-1}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The difference $(1+\theta)^n-1-(1-\theta)^n$ increases with $\theta$ and is
positive at $\theta=\frac1n$, at $\theta=\frac14$ for $n=3$ and at
$\theta=\frac3{13}$ for $n=4$; so $\rho_n>n$, $\rho_3>4$, $\rho_4>\frac{13}3$,
and with $\rho_1=2$, $\rho_2=4$ this gives the values of the minimum.

For the row $k\ge3$ in the second case, $n=n_k\ge2$, as otherwise
$T_k>10U_{k-1}>3\rho_1U_{k-1}$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} applies; put
$m=n+9$ and take $\alpha\in A_{k-1}$, so
$T_{k-1}\le|\alpha|\le U_{k-1}<T_k/m$.  In Theorem~\ref{thm:rowsk},
$\lambda>9/m$, $\gamma>(1-\frac1m)^n$, $1-1/|\alpha|>\frac{26}{27}$ (as
$|\alpha|\ge T_{k-1}>9U_{k-2}\ge9T_1>27$), and
$\xi<3/(n_{k-1}+9)\le3/(m+1)$, as $n_{k-1}\ge n+1$.  The bracket in
$\varepsilon$ increases with $\lambda$ and decreases with $\xi$, so it
exceeds
\[
  V=\frac9m\cdot\frac{m-5}{m+1}-\frac{m-9}m\cdot\frac3{m-2}
  =\frac{6m^2-39m+117}{m(m+1)(m-2)} ,
\]
and $\varepsilon>2^{-n-1}$ once $\gamma V\ge\frac{27}{26}2^{-n}$.  For $n\ge4$,
$V\ge3/m$ (as $m^2-12m+41>0$) and $\gamma\ge e^{-(m-9)/(m-1)}>e^{-1}$, and
$3/(e(n+9))\ge\frac{27}{26}2^{-n}$ holds at $n=4$ and so for all $n\ge4$.
For $n=2,3$, $\gamma V>(\frac{10}{11})^2\frac{23}{66}>0.28>\frac{27}{104}$ and
$\gamma V>(\frac{11}{12})^3\frac{171}{520}>0.25>\frac{27}{208}$.

For the first sentence, $n_a\ge2$ for $a<S$, so $T_a>(n_a+9)U_{a-1}\ge11U_{a-1}$
there, and the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} hold for every $k$.
The row $1$ is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}: Lemma~\ref{lem:central} at
$r=1$ needs no separation.  For $k\ge2$, if $T_k>3\rho_{n_k}U_{k-1}$ the row
$k$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow}; otherwise $n_k\ge2$ and
$T_k>(n_k+9)U_{k-1}$, and the row is given for $k=2$ by the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}, which uses only $T_1>3$,
$T_2>\max(9,n_2+1)U_1$ and $T_a\ge T_2$ for $a\ge2$, and for $k\ge3$ by the
second case.  The rows give the mass as in Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}.
\end{proof}

So the rows of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, need a separation
$n_a+O(1)$: they fail at $n_aU_{a-1}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}) and
hold at $\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})U_{a-1}$; for any $c>3$ the same computation gives the row $k$
at $T_{k-1}>(n_{k-1}+c)U_{k-2}$, $T_k>(n_k+c)U_{k-1}$ once $n_k$ is large.
Unlike the row $2$, the row $3$ needs more than the separation just below
it: at $T_3=(n_3+h)U_2$ it fails unless $T_2$ grows with $n_2$, as the zero
of the second annulus can then cancel the coefficient of $x^2$ together with
the zero below it.

\begin{proposition}[The row $3$ needs the lower separation]\label{prop:rowslower}
Let $h=u/v>0$ with coprime positive integers $u$ and $v$, let
$K\ge\max(3,18h)$ be an integer and $p\ge2^K$ a multiple of $v^2$, and put
$q_1=2h(K+h)p$, $q_2=K(K+1+2h)p$, $t=(K+h)q_2$ and
$F=(x-q_1)(x-q_2)(x+t)^K$.  Then Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} applies to the
monic $F\in\mathbb Z[x]$ with $S=3$, $A_1=\{q_1\}$, $A_2=\{q_2\}$ and $A_3$
the $K$-fold zero $-t$, so that $T_3=(n_3+h)U_2$ and
$T_2<(\frac{n_2}{2h}+1)U_1$, and
\[
  f_2=0,\qquad b_3(F)\ \le\ \frac{4t^K}{q_2}\ <\ \frac12\prod_{\beta\in A_3}\frac{|\beta|}2 .
\]
If moreover $h<\frac12$ and $(1-2h)K^2\ge1$, then also $T_2>(n_2+h)U_1$.
So the row $k=3$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, fails; as
$T_2/U_1\to\infty$ with $K$ while $b_3(F)/t^K\to0$ as $p\to\infty$, for no
$h$, $G$ and $\varepsilon>0$ do $T_3\ge(n_3+h)U_2$ and $T_2\ge GU_1$ give
$b_3(F)\ge\varepsilon|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A_3}|\beta|$; and for no
$c<\frac12$, $N$ and $\varepsilon>0$ do $n_3\ge N$ and
$T_a>(n_a+c)U_{a-1}$ for $a=2,3$ give it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $n_3=K$ and $n_2=K+1$, $T_3=U_3=t=(K+h)q_2$, and
$q_1=2u(Kv+u)p/v^2$, $q_2=K(Kv+v+2u)p/v$ and $t=(Kv+u)q_2/v$ are integers,
as $v^2\mid p$.  Further $T_2/U_1=q_2/q_1=K(K+1+2h)/(2h(K+h))$, which is below
$(K+1+2h)/(2h)=\frac{n_2}{2h}+1$ and above $9$, as $K\ge18h$ gives $K^2\ge18hK$
and $K+2hK>18h^2$; and $T_1=q_1\ge2u(Kv+u)>3$.  If $h<\frac12$, then
$K(K+1+2h)-2h(K+h)(K+1+h)=(1-2h)K(K+1+2h)-2h^2(1+h)>1-\frac34$, that is,
$T_2/U_1>K+1+h=n_2+h$.  With $(x+t)^K=\sum_i\binom Kit^{K-i}x^i$,
for $2\le i\le K+1$,
\[
  f_i=t^{K-i}\Bigl(\binom K{i-2}t^2-(q_1+q_2)\binom K{i-1}t+q_1q_2\binom Ki\Bigr).
\]
At $i=2$ the bracket is $t(t-Kq_2)-q_1(Kt-\binom K2q_2)
=h(K+h)q_2^2-q_1q_2K(K+1+2h)/2=0$.  For $i\ge3$, as $q_1<q_2$,
$\binom Kj\le K^j$ and $t\ge Kq_2$, the three terms of the bracket are at most
$t^iq_2^{2-i}$, $2t^iq_2^{2-i}$ and $t^iq_2^{2-i}$, so $|f_i|\le4t^Kq_2^{2-i}\le4t^K/q_2$.
The positions other than $0$ and $1$ thus have $|f_i|\le4t^K/q_2$, which
bounds $b_3(F)$, and $4/q_2<2^{-K-1}$ as $q_2\ge K(K+1)2^K\ge12\cdot2^K$.
For the last claim take a rational $h$ with $c<h<\frac12$ and $K\ge N$
with $(1-2h)K^2\ge1$, and let $p\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

In this family $\lambda=1-n_3U_2/T_3=h/(K+h)$, and the ratio $s=U_1/T_2$ has
$s/\lambda=2(K+h)^2/(K(K+1+2h))\to2$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:rowsk} needs
about $\lambda>\xi=3s$.  The loss is the central index at the radius just
above $3U_{k-2}$, which bounds the positions below $y_{k-1}$ by powers of
that radius rather than of the moduli in $A_{k-2}$.  A zero of $A_{k-2}$
determines the coefficient at $y_{k-2}$ from the one at $y_{k-1}$ instead,
and eliminating it leaves at $\alpha$ the truncation $S_M(\alpha)$, of
modulus at least $\lambda$, against $s$ times the next term of
$\prod(1-\alpha/\beta)^{-1}$, of modulus at most
$\binom{n+1}2(|\alpha|/T)^2\approx\frac12$: the condition becomes about
$s<2\lambda$, sharp to leading order by this family.

\begin{theorem}[The row $k$ at two lower zeros]\label{thm:rowspair}
With the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow}, let $k\ge3$, $n=n_k$,
$T=T_k$, $\Pi=\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}|\beta|$ and $\theta=2^{-n-1}$, and
let $r_1,\ldots,r_{k-1}$ be radii as in that lemma with $j=k-1$.  Let
$\alpha\in A_{k-1}$ with $a=|\alpha|<T/n$ and $\alpha'\in A_{k-2}$, and put
$\rho=a/T$, $\lambda=1-n\rho$, $s=|\alpha'|/a$, $\gamma=(1-\rho)^n$,
$\gamma'=(1-s\rho)^n$, $x_0=nr_{k-1}/T$, and $q=r_{k-2}/|\alpha'|$ if
$k\ge4$, $q=0$ if $k=3$.  Suppose $\theta<\gamma$ and
$2\gamma'-1>q/(1-q)$, and put
\[
  e=\frac{3\theta}{2\gamma(1-x_0)(1-\theta/\gamma)},\qquad
  \eta=\frac{1-\gamma'}{2\gamma'-1},\qquad
  \kappa=\frac{1+e}{2\gamma'-1-q/(1-q)},
\]
$\Delta=\max\bigl(\frac12n(n+1)\rho^2,s(\frac1\gamma-1)\bigr)$,
$\Xi=\min\bigl(\lambda(1-s)-s\Delta,1-\frac s\gamma\bigr)$ and
\[
  \nu=\frac s\gamma\Bigl(\eta+\frac{\kappa qs}{1-qs}+\frac{\kappa q}{(1-q)(2\gamma'-1)}\Bigr)
  +e\Bigl(1+\frac3{2(2\gamma'-1)}\Bigr).
\]
If $\Xi>\nu$, then $b_k(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Divide $F$ by $f_D$, so $f_D=1$ and the claim is $b_k(F)>\theta\Pi$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} gives central indices $y_a$ of $F$ at $r_a$ with
$y_1<\cdots<y_{k-1}$, $D-y_{k-1}\ge n$ and $|f_{y_a}|>\theta\Pi$.  Put
$y=y_{k-1}$, $y'=y_{k-2}$, $j=y-y'\ge1$, $r=r_{k-1}$ and $b=|\alpha'|=sa$,
and suppose that every other nonleading position $l$ has $|f_l|\le\theta\Pi$.
Let $H$, $P$, $d=D-n\ge y$, $w_m$ and $S_m$ be as in the case $n\ge2$ of the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo} (they need only $n\ge1$), $M=d-y$ and
$W_m=\binom{n+m-1}mT^{-m}$.  For $|z|<T$, $|S_m(z)|\le(1-|z|/T)^{-n}$ and
$|S_m(z)-1|\le(1-|z|/T)^{-n}-1$, so $|S_m(\alpha)|\le1/\gamma$ and
$2-1/\gamma'\le|S_m(\alpha')|\le1/\gamma'$.  As in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rowsk}, now with
$|f_l|W_{d-l}\le|f_y|r^{y-l}W_M(n/T)^{y-l}=|f_y|W_Mx_0^{y-l}$ for $l<y$, the
coefficient of $x^d$ gives $|f_y|W_M\ge(1-x_0)(1-\theta/\gamma)\Pi$, where
$x_0<\frac13$ as $r<T_{k-1}/3\le a/3$.  For
$z\in\{\alpha,\alpha'\}$, as $|z|\ge b\ge T_1>3$ and $W_M|z|^M\le(n|z|/T)^M\le1$,
\[
  \theta\Pi\sum_{l\le d}|z|^l\ <\ \frac{\theta\Pi|z|^{d+1}}{|z|-1}
  \ \le\ \frac{3\theta\Pi|z|^y}{2W_M}\ \le\ e\gamma|f_y||z|^y .
\]

For $z\in\{\alpha,\alpha'\}$, $H(0)P(z)=0$ reads
\[
  f_yz^yS_M(z)+f_{y'}z^{y'}S_{M+j}(z)+\Psi_z+E_z\ =\ 0,
\]
where, if $k\ge4$, $\Psi_z$ sums $f_lz^lS_{d-l}(z)$ over $l<y'$ and $E_z$
over $y'<l\le d$, $l\ne y$, and, if $k=3$, $\Psi_z=0$ and $E_z$ sums over
all $l\le d$ other than $y$ and $y'$.  The positions in $E_z$ are nonleading
and none of the $y_a$, so $|f_l|\le\theta\Pi$ there, and as
$|S_{d-l}(z)|\le1/\gamma$ the last display gives $|E_z|\le e|f_y||z|^y$.  If $k\ge4$, then $|f_l|\le|f_{y'}|r_{k-2}^{y'-l}$
for $l<y'$, as $y'$ is a central index at $r_{k-2}$.  At $z=\alpha'$ put
$Y=|f_y|b^y$ and $Z=|f_{y'}|b^{y'}$: then $|\Psi_{\alpha'}|\le
Zq/((1-q)\gamma')$, and the equation gives
$Z(2-1/\gamma')\le Y/\gamma'+Zq/((1-q)\gamma')+eY$, so $Z\le\kappa Y$, as
$\gamma'\le1$.

Multiply the equation at $\alpha'$ by
$\mu=(\alpha/\alpha')^{y'}S_{M+j}(\alpha)/S_{M+j}(\alpha')$ and subtract it
from the equation at $\alpha$.  With $t=(\alpha'/\alpha)^j$, so $|t|=s^j$,
and $\varphi=S_M(\alpha')/S_{M+j}(\alpha')$, so $|\varphi-1|\le\eta$, the
coefficient $f_{y'}$ cancels and
\[
  f_y\alpha^y\bigl(S_M(\alpha)(1-t)-t(S_{M+j}-S_M)(\alpha)-tS_{M+j}(\alpha)(\varphi-1)\bigr)
  \ =\ \mu(\Psi_{\alpha'}+E_{\alpha'})-\Psi_\alpha-E_\alpha .
\]
Here $|(S_{M+j}-S_M)(\alpha)|\le\sum_{m=M+1}^{M+j}W_ma^m$.  If $M=0$, then
$S_0=1$ and this sum is at most $\frac1\gamma-1$, so the bracket has modulus
at least $1-\frac s\gamma-\frac{s\eta}\gamma$.  If $M\ge1$, then
$|S_M(\alpha)|\ge\lambda$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}; as
$W_{m+1}a^{m+1}/(W_ma^m)=(n+m)\rho/(m+1)\le1$, the sum is at most
$W_2a^2=\frac12n(n+1)\rho^2$ if $j=1$, and if $j\ge2$ then $|t|\le s^2$ and
the sum is at most $\frac1\gamma-1$; so the bracket has modulus at least
$\lambda(1-s)-s\Delta-\frac{s\eta}\gamma$.  On the right,
$|E_\alpha|\le e|f_y|a^y$, and
$|f_{y'}|a^{y'}=Z(a/b)^{y'}\le\kappa|t||f_y|a^y\le\kappa s|f_y|a^y$, so, as
$r_{k-2}/a=qs$,
\[
  |\Psi_\alpha|\ \le\ \frac{|f_{y'}|a^{y'}}\gamma\sum_{i\ge1}(qs)^i
  \ \le\ \frac{\kappa s}\gamma\cdot\frac{qs}{1-qs}|f_y|a^y .
\]
As $|\mu|\le(a/b)^{y'}\gamma'/(\gamma(2\gamma'-1))$,
$|\mu\Psi_{\alpha'}|\le\frac{\kappa s}\gamma\cdot\frac q{(1-q)(2\gamma'-1)}|f_y|a^y$; and as
$a^{y'}b^{l-y'}\le a^l$ for $l>y'$ and $\sum_{l<y'}a^{y'}b^{l-y'}<a^{y'}/(b-1)<a^{d+1}/(2(a-1))$,
\[
  |\mu E_{\alpha'}|\ \le\ \frac{(a/b)^{y'}\theta\Pi}{\gamma(2\gamma'-1)}\sum_{l\le d,\,l\ne y,y'}b^l
  \ \le\ \frac{3\theta\Pi a^{d+1}}{2(a-1)\gamma(2\gamma'-1)}
  \ \le\ \frac{3e}{2(2\gamma'-1)}|f_y|a^y .
\]
So $\Xi-\frac{s\eta}\gamma\le\nu-\frac{s\eta}\gamma$, a contradiction.  So
some nonleading position other than the $y_a$ has $|f_l|>\theta\Pi$, and
$b_k(F)>\theta\Pi$.
\end{proof}

With the separation $n_a+c$ at every annulus, $\lambda$ and $s$ are about
$c/n_k$ and $1/n_k$, $\Delta$ is about $\frac12$ and $\nu=O(n_k^{-2})$, so
the condition $\Xi>\nu$ reads about $c>\frac12$.

\begin{corollary}[Rows at the separation $n_a+1$]\label{cor:rowsplusone}
Let $F$, $A_1,\ldots,A_S$, $K_s$ and $n_a$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}, except that of the moduli only $T_1>3$ is
assumed, and let $\rho_n$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $3\le k\le S$ with $n_k\ge30$.  If $T_a>(n_a+1)U_{a-1}$ for
$2\le a\le k$ and $T_a>U_{a-1}$ for $a>k$, then
$b_k(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge k}}\frac{|\beta|}2$.
\item For every $c>\frac12$ there is $N_c$ such that item~1 holds with
$n_k\ge N_c$ and $T_a>(n_a+c)U_{a-1}$ in place of $n_k\ge30$ and
$T_a>(n_a+1)U_{a-1}$; for $c<\frac12$ there is no such $N_c$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rowslower}).
\item If $T_a>\sigma(n_a)U_{a-1}$ for $2\le a\le S$, where $\sigma(n)=n+1$
for $n\ge30$ and $\sigma(n)=\min(n+9,3\rho_n)$ for $n<30$, then the rows and
the mass bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, hold.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
1.  Put $n=n_k$, so $n_{k-1}\ge n+1$ and $n_{k-2}\ge n+2$, and every
$T_a>31U_{a-1}$ for $a\le k$; so the assumptions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} hold.  Take $\alpha\in A_{k-1}$ and
$\alpha'\in A_{k-2}$.  Then $a\le U_{k-1}<T/(n+1)$, so $\rho<\frac1{n+1}$ and
$\lambda>\frac1{n+1}$, and $s\le U_{k-2}/T_{k-1}<\frac1{n+2}$.  Choose the
radii of that lemma with $r_{k-1}<T/(10n)$, possible as
$T>(n+1)(n+2)U_{k-2}>30nU_{k-2}$, and then $\rho_nr_{k-1}<\frac73nr_{k-1}<T$;
and, if $k\ge4$, with $r_{k-2}<3T_{k-2}/(n+3)$, possible as
$T_{k-2}>(n+3)U_{k-3}$.  So $x_0<\frac1{10}$ and $q<\frac3{n+3}$.  Now
$\gamma\ge(\frac n{n+1})^n>e^{-1}$, so $\frac1\gamma<3$ and $\theta<\gamma$;
by Bernoulli's inequality $\gamma'\ge1-ns\rho>1-\frac1{n+2}$, so
$2\gamma'-1>\frac n{n+2}\ge\frac{15}{16}$ and $\eta<\frac1n$; and
$q/(1-q)<\frac3n\le\frac1{10}$.  Hence $e<2^{2-n}$ and
$\kappa<(1+2^{-28})/(\frac{15}{16}-\frac1{10})<\frac65$.  Both terms of
$\Delta$ are below $\frac n{2(n+1)}$, so
$\lambda(1-s)-s\Delta>\frac1{n+2}-\frac n{2(n+1)(n+2)}=\frac1{2(n+1)}$, and
$\Xi>\frac1{2(n+1)}$.  As $qs<\frac3{(n+2)(n+3)}<\frac1{11n}$,
\[
  \nu\ <\ \frac3{n+2}\Bigl(\frac1n+\frac65\cdot\frac1{11n-1}+\frac65\cdot\frac3n\cdot\frac{16}{15}\Bigr)
  +2^{2-n}\cdot\frac{13}5\ <\ \frac{15}{n(n+2)}+2^{4-n},
\]
and $\frac1{2(n+1)}-\frac{15}{n(n+2)}=\frac{n^2-28n-30}{2n(n+1)(n+2)}\ge\frac{15}{n(n+1)(n+2)}>2^{4-n}$
for $n\ge30$, as $2^{26}>30\cdot31\cdot32/15$ and $n(n+1)(n+2)$ less than
doubles when $n$ grows by $1$.  So $\Xi>\nu$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rowspair} gives the row.

2.  With $n+c$ in place of $n+1$ the same choices give $\rho<\frac1{n+c}$,
$s<\frac1{n+1+c}$, $q<\frac3{n+2+c}$ and $x_0<\frac1{10}$ once $n$ is large.
Since $\gamma$ and $\gamma'$ decrease in $\rho$ and $s$, $\Xi$ decreases and
$\nu$ increases in $\rho$, $s$, $q$ and $x_0$, so it suffices that $\Xi>\nu$
at these bounds, where $\theta<\gamma$ and $2\gamma'-1>q/(1-q)$ for large
$n$.  There, as $n\to\infty$, $n\lambda\to c$, $ns\to1$,
$\Delta\to\frac12$, $\frac1\gamma\to e$, $\eta$, $q$ and $e$ tend to $0$,
and $\kappa\to1$; so $n\Xi\to c-\frac12>0$ and $n\nu\to0$.  For $c<\frac12$
see the last clause of Proposition~\ref{prop:rowslower}.

3.  The row $1$ is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}.  The row $2$ is
Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} if $T_2>3\rho_{n_2}U_1$, and otherwise the proof
of Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}, as $T_2>\max(9,n_2+1)U_1$ and every
$T_a>U_{a-1}$.  Let $k\ge3$.  For $a<k$, $n_a\ge2$, so $\sigma(n_a)>9$.  If
$n_k\ge30$, item~1 gives the row.  If $n_k<30$ and
$T_k>3\rho_{n_k}U_{k-1}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsbelow} does.  Otherwise
$2\le n_k\le29$ and $T_k>(n_k+9)U_{k-1}$.  If $n_{k-1}<30$, then
$T_{k-1}>(n_{k-1}+9)U_{k-2}$ and Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsall} gives the row.
If $n_{k-1}\ge30$, then $\xi<\frac3{31}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:rowsk}, which
is at most $\frac3{n_k+10}$ for $n_k\le21$, and the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsall} applies; for $22\le n_k\le29$,
$\lambda>\frac9{38}$, the bracket in $\varepsilon$ exceeds
$\frac9{38}\cdot\frac{25}{31}-\frac{29}{38}\cdot\frac3{28}>\frac1{10}$,
$\gamma>(1-\frac1{n_k+9})^{n_k}>e^{-1}$ and $1-1/|\alpha|>\frac{26}{27}$, so
$\varepsilon>\frac1{60}>2^{-n_k-1}$.  The rows give the mass as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}.
\end{proof}

So for the rows $k\ge3$ the additive constant in the separation $n_a+O(1)$
is $\frac12$ once $n_a$ is large: every $c>\frac12$ suffices and every
$c<\frac12$ fails, the failure needing both separations
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rowslower}); for the row $2$ every $c>0$ suffices
(the remark after Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}; for $S=2$ the excess over
$n_2$ is even exponentially small, Corollary~\ref{cor:twofixed}) and $c=0$
does not.  The threshold $30$ is not optimized.  Whether
$T_a>(n_a+1)U_{a-1}$ for every $a$ gives all the rows also when $n_a<30$ is
open for $S\ge3$; there Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsall} gives $n_a+9$, and $6$
at $n_a=1$.

For the mass the cancellation of Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn}, with $K=2$, breaks
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} at the separation $2$.

\begin{proposition}[Separation $2$ does not suffice]\label{prop:gaptwo}
Let $q\ge97$ be an integer, $\delta=1/(2q+1)$, $t=2q+\delta$ and
$F=(x-q)(x+t)^2$.  With $S=2$, $A_1=\{q\}$ and $A_2$ the double zero $-t$,
$T_1=U_1=q>3$ and $T_2=U_2=t=\bigl(2+\frac1{q(2q+1)}\bigr)U_1$, but
\[
  \sum_{s=1}^2s\sum_{\beta\in A_s}\log\frac{|\beta|}2-2\log2-\Lambda(F)
  \ =\ \log\frac{t^2}{128(3q+2\delta)}\ >\ 0 ,
\]
and the difference tends to $\infty$ with $q$.  So
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} fails at the separation $2$, by an unbounded
amount, and, taking $q=97$, its factor $81$ cannot be replaced by any
$g<2+\frac1{18915}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F=x^3+(2t-q)x^2+t(t-2q)x-qt^2$, and $t(t-2q)=t\delta=(2q+\delta)/(2q+1)$
lies in $(0,1)$, so $\Lambda(F)=\log(qt^2)+\log(3q+2\delta)$.  The bound is
$\log(q/2)+4\log(t/2)-2\log2=\log(qt^4/128)$, and the difference is as
stated.  As $t>2q$ and $\delta<1$, $t^2>4q^2>128(3q+2)$ once
$q^2-96q-64>0$, which holds for $q\ge97$; and $t^2/(3q+2\delta)\to\infty$.
Finally $t/q=2+\delta/q$, which is $2+\frac1{18915}$ at $q=97$.
\end{proof}

With few roots above a gap the single central index of
Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, needs less than the factor $9n_a$: by
the last clause of Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}, $T_a>3\rho_{n_a}U_{a-1}$
suffices, and $3\rho_{n_a}<81$ for $n_a\le13$.  At a gap where it does not
suffice the two positions of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} can be used, gap by gap.

\begin{corollary}[Few roots above a gap]\label{cor:fewabove}
Let $F$, $A_1,\ldots,A_S$, $K_s$ and $n_a$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli}, except that only $T_1>3$ is assumed of the
moduli, and let $\rho_n$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $T_a>3\rho_{n_a}U_{a-1}$ for $2\le a\le S$, in particular if
$T_a>7n_aU_{a-1}$, the conclusions of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1,
hold.
\item If $|f_D|\ge1$ and, for each $2\le a\le S$, $T_a>3\rho_{n_a}U_{a-1}$ or
$T_a>81U_{a-1}$, the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} holds.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for $S=2$ the mass bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} holds
once $T_2>6U_1$ if $K_2=1$, and once $T_2>12U_1$ if $K_2=2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each $a$ pick $\beta\in A_a$ and, by Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton},
item~2, a tropical root $\sigma^{(a)}$ with
$\log(T_a/3)\le\sigma^{(a)}\le\log(3U_a)$.  Let $y_1$ be a central index at
$1<e^{\sigma^{(1)}}$ and $Y_1=\{y_1\}$.  For $2\le a\le S$, if
$T_a>3\rho_{n_a}U_{a-1}$ let $Y_a=\{y_a\}$, $y_a$ a central index at a radius
$r_a$ with $3U_{a-1}<r_a<T_a/\rho_{n_a}$; Lemma~\ref{lem:tropcount} with
$A=A_{\ge a}$ gives $D-y_a\ge n_a$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:central} gives
$|f_{y_a}|>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_{\ge a}}\frac{|\beta|}2$.  Otherwise
$T_a>81U_{a-1}$, and let $Y_a$ be the set of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap}, whose positions are the vertices active at
$u_a^\pm$, with $\log(3U_{a-1})<u_a^-<u_a^+<\sigma^{(a)}$; there
$\sum_{y\in Y_a}\log^+|f_y|\ge\log|f_D|+\sum_{\beta\in A_{\ge a}}\log(|\beta|/2)-\log2$.

Every position of $Y_a$, $a\ge2$, is a central index at a radius above
$3U_{a-1}\ge e^{\sigma^{(a-1)}}$ and, unless $a=S$, below $e^{\sigma^{(a)}}$:
if $n_a\ge2$ then $\rho_{n_a}\ge4$ and $r_a<T_a/4<e^{\sigma^{(a)}}$, and
$n_a=1$ only for $a=S$.  As in the Order step of Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli},
a central index at a radius below $e^{\sigma^{(a)}}$ lies below every central
index at a radius above it; so every position of $Y_a$ lies below every
position of $Y_{a+1}$.  The positions of $Y_S$ lie below $D$: $D-y_S\ge n_S$
in the first case, and by the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} in the
second.  So the positions in $Y_1\cup\cdots\cup Y_S$ are distinct and
nonleading, and the rest is as in the proofs of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli},
item~1, and of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap}.  For the last sentence,
$3\rho_1=6$ and $3\rho_2=12$.
\end{proof}

So for the mass at two annuli with two roots in the upper one, the case of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gaptwo}, the least separation lies between
$2+\frac1{18915}$ and $12$, by Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo} even $9$, and
by Corollaries~\ref{cor:twofixed} and~\ref{cor:twonear} below even $4$ and
$\frac52$; with one root in the upper annulus it is at
least $\frac{32}{31}$, since $F=(x-q)(x+t)$ has
$\Lambda(F)=\log(qt(t-q))$ against the bound $\log(qt^2/32)$ when
$1\le t-q<t/32$, for instance at $q=100$, $t=103$.  For the rows,
Proposition~\ref{prop:rowsneedn} and Corollaries~\ref{cor:fewabove}
and~\ref{cor:rowsall} put the least separation between $n_a$ and
$\min(n_a+9,3\rho_{n_a})$, which is $3\rho_1=6$ for $n_a=1$ and $n_a+9$ for
$n_a\ge2$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:rowsplusone} lowers it to $n_a+1$ where
$n_a\ge30$.

For two annuli the charge of $A_2$ needs one position besides the central
index at $1$.  The truncation argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo} can
exclude any given position, and splitting its two identities instead of
bounding one by the other roughly halves the constant; at small separations
this matters, and it gives the rows and the mass at a fixed separation when
few roots lie above the gap.

\begin{lemma}[A second position below the gap]\label{lem:twobelow}
Let $F$, $A$, $n$, $T$ and $\alpha$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo}, and
put $a=|\alpha|$, $\lambda=1-na/T$, $\Pi=\prod_{\beta\in A}|\beta|$ and
\[
  \Gamma=\Bigl(1-\frac aT\Bigr)^{-n}\frac a{(a-1)\lambda}+\Bigl(1-\frac1T\Bigr)^{-n}.
\]
For every position $j$ some position $l\le D-n$ with $l\ne j$ has
$|f_l|\ge|f_D|\Pi/\Gamma$.  In particular $b_2(F)\ge|f_D|\Pi/\Gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $H$, $P=F/H$, $d=D-n$, $w_k$ and $S_m$ be as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo}; $P$ has leading coefficient $f_D$ and
$P(\alpha)=0$, so $d\ge1$.  The coefficient of $x^d$ in
$H(0)P(x)=\sum_{l\le d}f_lx^lS_{d-l}(x)$, and its value at $\alpha$, give
\[
  H(0)f_D=\sum_{l\le d}f_lw_{d-l},\qquad0=\sum_{l\le d}f_l\alpha^lS_{d-l}(\alpha),
\]
where $\sum_k|w_k|\le\sum_k\binom{n+k-1}kT^{-k}=(1-1/T)^{-n}$,
$|S_m(\alpha)|\le(1-a/T)^{-n}$, and $|S_m(\alpha)|\ge\lambda$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}.  Let $m$ be the largest $|f_l|$ over $l\le d$,
$l\ne j$.  If $j>d$, the first identity gives
$|f_D|\Pi\le m(1-1/T)^{-n}\le m\Gamma$.  Let $j\le d$ and $M=d-j$.  The
second identity gives
\[
  |f_j|a^j\lambda\ \le\ \Bigl(1-\frac aT\Bigr)^{-n}\sum_{l\le d,\,l\ne j}|f_l|a^l
  \ <\ m\Bigl(1-\frac aT\Bigr)^{-n}\frac{a^{d+1}}{a-1},
\]
and $a^M|w_M|\le\binom{n+M-1}M(a/T)^M\le(na/T)^M\le1$, as
$\binom{n+M-1}M\le n^M$.  So the first identity gives
$|f_D|\Pi\le|f_j||w_M|+m(1-1/T)^{-n}\le m\Gamma$.  For $b_2(F)$ take for
$j$ a position of a largest nonleading coefficient; then $l\ne j$ and
$l<D$.
\end{proof}

For $n\ge2$, with $\gamma=(1-a/T)^n$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo},
$\Gamma<a/((a-1)\lambda\gamma)+1/\gamma$, against $2a/((a-1)\lambda\gamma)$
there: the same order in $\lambda$, with about half the constant.  For $n=1$,
$\Gamma$ is of order $\lambda^{-2}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo}, which
uses both zeros, is better for small $\lambda$.  What the lemma adds is the
free choice of $j$ and the constant: at $T_2=4U_1$ with $n_2=2$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:rowstwo} guarantees only
$b_2(F)>\frac{3}{32}|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A_2}|\beta|$, below the row
$\frac18|f_D|\prod_{\beta\in A_2}|\beta|$.

\begin{corollary}[Two annuli at a fixed separation]\label{cor:twofixed}
Let $F$, $A_1$, $A_2$ and $n_2=K_2$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} with
$S=2$, except that only $T_1>3$ is assumed of the moduli.  Let $g=4$ if
$n_2\le3$ and $g=n_2(1+3\cdot2^{-n_2})$ if $n_2\ge4$, so that
$g\le\max(4,n_2+1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $T_2>gU_1$, then
$b_2(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{\beta\in A_2}\frac{|\beta|}2$, and the rows and
the mass bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, hold.
\item Let $K\ge1$, $q\ge4^{K+1}$ and $t=Kq/(1-2^{-K-1})$.  Then
$F=(x-q)(x+t)^K$, with $A_1=\{q\}$ and $A_2$ the $K$-fold zero $-t$, has
$T_1=U_1=q$ and $T_2=t=KU_1/(1-2^{-K-1})$, but
$b_2(F)\le\frac12\prod_{\beta\in A_2}\frac{|\beta|}2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
1.  Take $\alpha\in A_1$, so $3<a=|\alpha|\le U_1$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:twobelow} with $A=A_2$, $n=n_2$ and $T=T_2$; as $g>n$,
$a<T/n$.  Since $a/(a-1)<\frac32$, $a/T<1/g$, $\lambda>1-n/g$ and
$T>gT_1>3g$,
\[
  \Gamma\ <\ \gamma_n=\frac32\Bigl(1-\frac1g\Bigr)^{-n}\frac g{g-n}+\Bigl(1-\frac1{3g}\Bigr)^{-n},
\]
and it remains that $\gamma_n\le2^{n+1}$: then
$b_2(F)\ge|f_D|\Pi/\Gamma>|f_D|\Pi2^{-n-1}$.  For $n\le3$, $g=4$ and
$\gamma_1=\frac83+\frac{12}{11}<4$, $\gamma_2=\frac{16}3+(\frac{12}{11})^2<8$,
$\gamma_3=\frac{128}9+(\frac{12}{11})^3<16$.  For $n\ge4$,
$g/(g-n)=1+2^n/3$, and as $(1-\frac1n)^{-n}$ and $(1-\frac1{3n})^{-n}$
decrease with $n$ (their logarithms are $\sum_kn^{1-k}/k$ and
$\sum_kn^{1-k}/(k3^k)$), $(1-\frac1g)^{-n}\le(\frac43)^4<\frac{19}6$ and
$(1-\frac1{3g})^{-n}\le(\frac{12}{11})^4<\frac{17}{12}$; so
$\gamma_n<\frac{19}4(1+\frac{2^n}3)+\frac{17}{12}=\frac{19}{12}2^n+\frac{37}6$,
which is at most $2^{n+1}$ as $\frac5{12}2^n\ge\frac{20}3>\frac{37}6$.  The
row $k=1$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:central} at $r=1$ with $A=A_1\cup A_2$, and the
two rows give the mass as in Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}.

2.  Here $f_1=t^{K-1}(t-Kq)=2^{-K-1}t^K$, and for $2\le i\le K$,
$f_i=\binom K{i-1}t^{K-i+1}-q\binom Kit^{K-i}$, so
$|f_i|<2^Kt^{K-1}+2^Kqt^{K-2}\le2^{K+1}t^{K-1}\le2^{-K-1}t^K$, as $q<t$ and
$t>4^{K+1}$.  So every nonleading coefficient but $f_0$ has modulus at most
$2^{-K-1}t^K=\frac12\prod_{\beta\in A_2}\frac{|\beta|}2$, which bounds $b_2(F)$.
\end{proof}

So for two annuli the second row, and with it the mass bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap}, holds at the separation $4$ with at most three
roots above the gap, and at $n_2+3n_22^{-n_2}$ otherwise, while the rows
need $n_2/(1-2^{-n_2-1})>n_2+n_22^{-n_2-1}$ (item~2 with $K=n_2$): the
least separation for the rows lies between these, and exceeds $n_2$ by an
amount exponentially small in $n_2$.  For the mass with $K_2=1$ or $K_2=2$
the least separation lies in $[\frac{32}{31},\frac{14}9]$ and
$[2+\frac1{18915},\frac52]$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:twonear} below).  At a fixed separation $g$ and $n_2=K\ge g$ two
nonleading positions no longer suffice: by the proof of item~2,
$F=(x-q)(x+t)^K$ with $t=Kq/(1-2^{-2K-4})$ and $q\ge2^{3K+5}$ has
$b_1(F)=qt^K$ and $b_2(F)\le2^{-2K-4}t^K$, so
$\log b_1(F)+\log b_2(F)$ falls short of the mass bound
$\log(q/2)+2K\log(t/2)-2\log2$ by at least $\log2$, and the positions
$2,\ldots,K$ carry the rest.  So a mass bound at a fixed separation for
all $n_2$ must charge three or more nonleading positions.

With one or two roots above the gap the least separation is close to its
lower bound.  Lemma~\ref{lem:twobelow} eliminates the positions above
$D-n$ and pays for it with the factor $\lambda$; eliminating instead the
position $j$ between one zero below the gap and one above charges that zero
above to the position $D-1$, or with a higher power of $|\beta|$ to a lower
one, with no factor $\lambda$ at all.

\begin{lemma}[Two zeros and one excluded position]\label{lem:twozeros}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be
zeros of $F$ with $1<a=|\alpha|<b=|\beta|$, put $\rho=a/b$, and let $j<D$
be a position.  Then
\[
  |f_D|\,b\,(1-\rho^{D-j})\ \le\ \sum_{j<l<D}|f_l|(1+\rho^{l-j})b^{l-D+1}
  +\sum_{l<j}|f_l|\bigl(a^{l-j}+b^{l-j}\bigr)b^{j-D+1} .
\]
Hence if $j=D-1$, some $l<D-1$ has
$|f_l|(\frac1{a-1}+\frac1{b-1})\ge|f_D|b(1-\rho)$; and if $j\le D-2$,
then, with $m$ the largest $|f_l|$ over $l\le D-2$, $l\ne j$ ($m=0$ if
there is none),
\[
  |f_D|\,b\,(1-\rho^2)\ \le\ (1+\rho)|f_{D-1}|
  +\Bigl(\frac{1+\rho}{b-1}+\frac1{b(a-1)}\Bigr)m .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $z=\alpha/\beta$.  Subtracting $z^j\sum_lf_l\beta^l=0$ from
$\sum_lf_l\alpha^l=0$ and dividing by $z^j$ gives
$\sum_lf_l\beta^l(z^{l-j}-1)=0$, whose term $l=j$ vanishes, so
\[
  f_D\beta^D(1-z^{D-j})=\sum_{l\ne j,D}f_l\beta^l(z^{l-j}-1).
\]
The left side has modulus at least $|f_D|b^D(1-\rho^{D-j})$.  On the right,
$|z^{l-j}-1|\le1+\rho^{l-j}$ for $l>j$, and
$b^l|z^{l-j}-1|\le b^l\rho^{l-j}+b^l=b^ja^{l-j}+b^l$ for $l<j$; dividing
by $b^{D-1}$ gives the inequality.  If $j=D-1$, only the second sum is
present, and its weights add up to less than $\frac1{a-1}+\frac1{b-1}$.  If
$j\le D-2$, then $1-\rho^{D-j}\ge1-\rho^2$, the weight of $l=D-1$ is at most
$1+\rho$, and as $b^{j-D+1}\le b^{-1}$ the weights of the positions $l\le
D-2$ add up to less than $(1+\rho)\sum_{k\ge1}b^{-k}+b^{-1}\sum_{k\ge1}a^{-k}$.
\end{proof}

At $F=(x-q)(x+q+1)$, $q>1$, and $j=0$ the inequality is an equality.

\begin{corollary}[One or two roots above the gap]\label{cor:twonear}
Let $F$, $A_1$, $A_2$ and $n_2=K_2$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:annuli} with
$S=2$, except that only $T_1>3$ is assumed of the moduli.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $K_2=1$ and $T_2>(\frac43+\frac2{3T_1})U_1$, then
$b_2(F)>\frac{|f_D|}2\cdot\frac{|\beta|}2$ for the zero $\beta$ of $A_2$, and
the rows and the mass bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:annuli}, item~1, hold.
The factor $\frac43$ cannot be lowered: for $3<q<t<\frac43q$,
$F=(x-q)(x+t)$ has $b_2(F)=t-q<\frac t4$.
\item If $K_2=2$, $|f_D|\ge1$ and $T_2>gU_1$ with
$g=\min(\frac52,2+\frac2{T_1})$, the mass bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\alpha\in A_1$ and put $a=|\alpha|$ and $\tau=T_1$, so
$3<\tau\le a\le U_1$; let $\beta$ be a zero of $A_2$ of the largest modulus
$b$, so $b\ge T_2>ga\ge g\tau$ for the relevant $g$, and $\rho=a/b<1/g$.

1.  Here $g=\frac43+\frac2{3\tau}=\frac{4\tau+2}{3\tau}$.  Let $j$ be a
position of a largest nonleading coefficient, and suppose that
$|f_l|\le|f_D|b/4$ for every $l\ne j,D$, and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:twozeros}.  If $j=D-1$, it gives
$4(1-\rho)\le\frac1{a-1}+\frac1{b-1}<\frac12+\frac13$, as $b>g\tau\ge4$;
but $4(1-\rho)>4(1-\frac1g)\ge1$.  If $j\le D-2$, it gives
$4(1-\rho^2)\le(1+\rho)(1+\frac1{b-1})+\frac1{b(a-1)}$, so
$4(1-\rho)-1\le\frac1{b-1}+\frac1{b(a-1)}$.  Here
$4(1-\rho)-1>3-\frac4g=\frac3{2\tau+1}$, while
$\frac1{b-1}<\frac1{g\tau-1}=\frac3{4\tau-1}$ and
$\frac1{b(a-1)}<\frac1{g\tau(\tau-1)}=\frac3{(4\tau+2)(\tau-1)}$, and
\[
  \frac3{2\tau+1}-\frac3{4\tau-1}-\frac3{(4\tau+2)(\tau-1)}
  =\frac{3(2\tau-1)(2\tau-5)}{2(2\tau+1)(4\tau-1)(\tau-1)}\ >\ 0 .
\]
Both cases are contradictory, so some $l\ne j,D$ has $|f_l|>|f_D|b/4$,
which bounds $b_2(F)$.  The row $k=1$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:central} at $r=1$
with $A=A_1\cup A_2$, and the two rows give the mass as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:rowstwo}.  Finally $(x-q)(x+t)=x^2+(t-q)x-qt$ has
$T_1=U_1=q$, $T_2=t$ and $b_2=t-q$.

2.  It suffices to treat $g=\frac52$ and $g=2+\frac2\tau$ separately.  Let
$\Pi$ be the product of the moduli of the two zeros of $A_2$, so
$\Pi\le b^2$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:central} at $r=1$ with $A=A_1\cup A_2$, a
central index at $1$ has modulus above
$\frac{|f_D|}2\prod_{A_1\cup A_2}\frac{|\beta|}2\ge|f_D|aT_2^2/16>|f_D|$, so
it is not $D$, and we take it for $j$.  First, $j\le D-2$.  Otherwise $j=D-1$ carries the largest value of the
concave majorant, so the edge from $D-1$ to $D$ has the tropical root
$\sigma_1=\log(|f_{D-1}|/|f_D|)>0$ and every other tropical root is at most
$0$.  If $\sigma_1>2\log3$, take $u$ with $\log3<u<\min(\log a,\sigma_1-\log3)$:
it is farther than $\log3$ from every tropical root, the vertex active at $u$
is $D-1$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~1, puts $D-1$ zeros in
$|z|<e^u$, although $\alpha$ and the two zeros of $A_2$ lie outside.  So
$|f_{D-1}|\le9|f_D|$, against $|f_{D-1}|>|f_D|aT_2^2/16>|f_D|g^2\tau^3/16$,
as $g^2\tau^3\ge144$: it exceeds $27\cdot\frac{25}4$ for $g=\frac52$, and is
$4\tau(\tau+1)^2>192$ for $g=2+\frac2\tau$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:twobelow} with $A=A_2$, $n=2$ and $T=T_2$ applies, as $a<T_2/2$;
let $m$ be the largest $|f_l|$ over $l\le D-2$, $l\ne j$, so
$m\ge|f_D|\Pi/\Gamma$.  If $m\ge|f_D|\Pi/8$, its position alone carries
$\log(|f_D|/2)+\sum_{A_2}\log(|\beta|/2)$.  Otherwise
Lemma~\ref{lem:twozeros} with $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $j\le D-2$ gives
$(1+\rho)|f_{D-1}|>|f_D|b(1-\rho^2)-|f_D|b^2W/8$, with $W$ its weight
$\frac{1+\rho}{b-1}+\frac1{b(a-1)}$, so $|f_{D-1}|>|f_D|b\kappa$ with
\[
  \kappa=1-\frac1g-\frac18\Bigl(\frac{g\tau}{g\tau-1}+\frac1{\tau-1}\Bigr)
  \ \le\ 1-\rho-\frac18\Bigl(\frac b{b-1}+\frac1{a-1}\Bigr),
\]
and then the positions $D-1$ and that of $m$, distinct from each other and
from $j$, carry $\log(|f_{D-1}|m)\ge\log(|f_D|\Pi/8)$ as soon as $g\tau\kappa\ge\Gamma/8$, as
$|f_D|\ge1$ and $b>g\tau$.  By the bounds on $a$ and $T_2$,
\[
  \Gamma\ <\ \gamma=\Bigl(1-\frac1g\Bigr)^{-2}\frac{\tau}{(\tau-1)(1-2/g)}
  +\Bigl(1-\frac1{g\tau}\Bigr)^{-2}.
\]
For $g=\frac52$, as $g\tau>\frac{15}2$ and $\tau>3$,
$\kappa\ge\frac35-\frac18(\frac{15}{13}+\frac12)=\frac{409}{1040}$ and
$g\tau\kappa>\frac{1227}{416}>2.9$, while
$\gamma<\frac{125}6+(\frac{15}{13})^2<22.2<8\cdot2.9$.  For
$g=2+\frac2\tau$, $1-\frac1g=\frac{\tau+2}{2\tau+2}$, $1-\frac2g=\frac1{\tau+1}$
and $g\tau=2\tau+2$, so, as $\frac{2\tau+2}{2\tau+1}\le\frac87$ and
$\frac{\tau+1}{\tau-1}\le2$,
\[
  g\tau\kappa=\tau+2-\frac{\tau+1}4\Bigl(\frac{2\tau+2}{2\tau+1}+\frac1{\tau-1}\Bigr)
  \ \ge\ \frac{10\tau+17}{14},
\]
while $\gamma=\frac{4\tau(\tau+1)^3}{(\tau+2)^2(\tau-1)}+(\frac{2\tau+2}{2\tau+1})^2
<4(\tau+3)+\frac{64}{49}$, as $\frac{\tau(\tau+1)}{\tau-1}=\tau+2+\frac2{\tau-1}$;
and $\frac{10\tau+17}{14}-\frac{\tau+3}2-\frac8{49}=\frac{3\tau-4}{14}-\frac8{49}>0$.

In both cases the positions other than $j$ and $D$ carry at least
$\log(|f_D|/2)+\sum_{A_2}\log(|\beta|/2)$, and $j$ carries
$\log(|f_D|/2)+\sum_{A_1\cup A_2}\log(|\beta|/2)$; with $\log^+|f_D|\ge\log|f_D|$
this is the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} for $S=2$.
\end{proof}

So with one root above the gap the second row needs a separation between
$\frac43$ and $\frac43+\frac2{3T_1}\le\frac{14}9$, and $\frac43$ is its limit
as $T_1\to\infty$; the mass needs between $\frac{32}{31}$ and the same.  With
two roots above the gap the mass holds at $\frac52$, and at $2+\frac2{T_1}$,
while Proposition~\ref{prop:gaptwo} breaks it at $2+\frac1{q(2q+1)}$ for
integers $T_1=q\ge97$: the least separation lies in $[2+\frac1{18915},\frac52]$,
and tends to $2$ as $T_1\to\infty$, the upper bound exceeding $2$ by order
$1/T_1$ and the lower by order $1/T_1^2$.  The argument does not extend to
many roots above the gap: Lemma~\ref{lem:twobelow} charges $k$ zeros above
the gap to one position only at a separation above $k$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:twozeros} charges only one; at a separation $g\le n_2$ the
$n_2$ zeros must be charged to several positions, as in $(x-q)(x+t)^K$ near
$t=Kq$, whose positions $i=2,\ldots,K$ carry $|f_i|$ of order
$\binom K{i-1}t^{K+1-i}$.

Below the separation $81$ the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedgap} breaks
down where only one vertex above those charged for the lower annuli is
active in the gap, between two tropical roots less than $2\log3$ apart, and
it has fewer than $n_a$ positions above it: then
Theorem~\ref{thm:archnewton}, item~1, gives no count, and there is no
second vertex to share the charge.  This is what happens in
Proposition~\ref{prop:gaptwo}: the vertices are $0$, $2$ and $3$, the
central index at $1$ is $0$, and above it only the vertex $2$, between the
tropical roots $\log(t/\sqrt{3+2\delta/q})$ and $\log(3q+2\delta)$, carries
the charge of the two zeros $-t$, with one position above it.

Open: the least fixed separation for the mass, between $2+\frac1{18915}$
and $81$, in particular whether $9$ suffices; for two annuli it is at most
$\max(4,n_2+1)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:twofixed}), and $\frac52$ for
$n_2\le2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:twonear}), and whether a separation
below $81$ independent of $n_2$ suffices there is open.

\section{Real roots of both signs}\label{sec:bothsigns}

\cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass} needs its roots on one side of $0$.  Applied to $F(x)$
and to $F(-x)$ it charges a multiple with $p$ positive and $n$ negative
prescribed roots only for the larger of the two classes.  The mixed problem is
weaker: by the multisection transfer (the remark after
Corollary~\ref{cor:imag}, with $R=\prod_j(y-s_j^2)$), the $L=2q$ roots $\pm s_j$
of $\prod_j(x^2-s_j^2)$ are worth only the $q$ positive roots $s_j^2$, so at a
common modulus $s$ they cost about $\frac14L^2\log s$ instead of the
$\frac12L^2\log s$ of $L$ roots of one sign.  The operator $T_z$ of the
proof of \cite[Proposition~4.2]{coefficient-mass}, where Rolle's theorem is
used on $(0,\tfrac12]$ only, here used on the two half-lines separately,
gives the matching lower bound: every excluded coefficient costs one root of
each sign.

\begin{lemma}[Rolle on both half-lines]\label{lem:rolleside}
Let $h\in\R[t]$ be nonzero, let $z\ge1$ be an integer, and put
$T_zh=h-(t/z)h'$, so that $[t^d]T_zh=(1-d/z)[t^d]h$.  If the zeros of $h$ in
$(0,\infty)$, listed with multiplicity, are $\xi_1\le\cdots\le\xi_m$, $m\ge1$, then $T_zh$ has
at least $m-1$ zeros in $(0,\infty)$, counted with multiplicity, and $m-1$ of
them, $\eta_1\le\cdots\le\eta_{m-1}$, satisfy $\xi_j\le\eta_j\le\xi_{j+1}$.
The same holds in $(-\infty,0)$ for the
absolute values: zeros $|\xi_1|\le\cdots\le|\xi_m|$ of $h$ there give
$m-1$ zeros $|\eta_1|\le\cdots\le|\eta_{m-1}|$ of $T_zh$ there with
$|\xi_j|\le|\eta_j|\le|\xi_{j+1}|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $t\ne0$, $T_zh=-(t^{z+1}/z)(t^{-z}h)'$.  On $I=(0,\infty)$ the function
$\varphi=t^{-z}h$ is real-analytic, not identically zero, and has the zeros of
$h$ with the same multiplicities.  Let $x_1<\cdots<x_q$ be the distinct zeros,
of multiplicities $\mu_i$.  Writing $\varphi=(t-x_i)^{\mu_i}\psi$ with
$\psi(x_i)\ne0$ shows that $\varphi'$ vanishes to order $\mu_i-1$ at $x_i$,
and Rolle's theorem gives a zero $y_i\in(x_i,x_{i+1})$ for $i<q$.  List
$\mu_1-1$ copies of $x_1$, then $y_1$, then $\mu_2-1$ copies of $x_2$, then
$y_2$, and so on: these are $m-1$ zeros of $\varphi'$ counted with
multiplicity, the $j$-th lies in $[\xi_j,\xi_{j+1}]$, and since
$-t^{z+1}/z$ has no zero in $I$ they are zeros of $T_zh$.  For
$(-\infty,0)$ apply this to $h(-t)$, using $(T_zh)(-t)=T_z\bigl(h(-t)\bigr)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rows at real roots of both signs]\label{thm:bothsigns}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero of degree $D$, and let
$a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_p>0$ and $c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_n>0$, repetitions allowed, with
$p+n\ge1$, be such that $\prod_{i}(x-a_i)\prod_i(x+c_i)$ divides $F$.  Let
$0<\rho<1$ with $\rho a_i>1$ and $\rho c_i>1$ for every $i$.  For
$1\le k\le\max(p,n)$ put
\[
  \Pi_k(\rho)=\prod_{i=k}^{p}(\rho a_i)\prod_{i=k}^{n}(\rho c_i),
\]
an empty product being $1$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bothrowsbound}
  b_k(F)\ \ge\ |f_D|\,\frac{\Pi_k(\rho)-1}{(1-\rho)^{-k}-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First let $F$ be real.  Since $k\le\max(p,n)\le p+n\le D$, there are at least
$k-1$ positions below $D$.  Let $z_1<\cdots<z_{k-1}$ be the backward distances
$D-j$ of $k-1$ positions $j<D$ carrying the $k-1$ largest nonleading
magnitudes; they are distinct positive integers, so $z_i\ge i$.  Let
$F^*(t)=t^DF(1/t)=\sum_{d=0}^Df_{D-d}t^d$ and
\[
  g=T_{z_{k-1}}\cdots T_{z_1}F^*,\qquad
  [t^d]g=f_{D-d}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\Bigl(1-\frac d{z_i}\Bigr).
\]
So $g(0)=f_D\ne0$ and $[t^{z_i}]g=0$.  As in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rowone}, $(x-\alpha)\mid F$ gives $(1-\alpha t)\mid F^*$,
so $F^*$ has the zeros $1/a_1\le\cdots\le1/a_p$ in $(0,\infty)$ and the zeros
$-1/c_i$, of absolute values $1/c_1\le\cdots\le1/c_n$, in $(-\infty,0)$, with
multiplicity.  Every intermediate polynomial $T_{z_s}\cdots T_{z_1}F^*$ has
constant term $f_D\ne0$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:rolleside} applies at each step;
after $s$ steps the $j$-th smallest positive zero is at most $\eta_j$, hence
at most the $(j+1)$-th smallest positive zero of the previous stage, hence at most $1/a_{j+s}$.  So $g$ has positive
zeros $\eta_j\le1/a_{j+k-1}$, $1\le j\le p-k+1$, and negative zeros
$\eta'_j$ with $|\eta'_j|\le1/c_{j+k-1}$, $1\le j\le n-k+1$, all of modulus
less than $\rho$.  Jensen's formula for $g$ on $|t|\le\rho$ gives
\[
  \log|f_D|+\log\Pi_k(\rho)\ \le\
  \log|f_D|+\sum_{g(\zeta)=0,\ |\zeta|<\rho}\log\frac\rho{|\zeta|}\ \le\
  \log\max_{|t|=\rho}|g(t)|,
\]
because $\log(\rho/|\eta_j|)\ge\log(\rho a_{j+k-1})$, likewise for the
$\eta'_j$, and the remaining terms are positive.  On the other side, for
$d\ge1$ either $[t^d]g=0$ or the position $D-d$ is not among the $k-1$ largest,
so $|f_{D-d}|\le b_k(F)$, while
$\prod_i|1-d/z_i|\le\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(1+d/i)=\binom{d+k-1}{k-1}$.  Hence
\[
  \max_{|t|=\rho}|g(t)|\ \le\ |f_D|+b_k(F)\sum_{d\ge1}\binom{d+k-1}{k-1}\rho^d
  =|f_D|+b_k(F)\bigl((1-\rho)^{-k}-1\bigr),
\]
and the two displays give \eqref{eq:bothrowsbound}.  For complex $F$ apply the
real case to $\operatorname{Re}(F/f_D)$, as in \cite[Corollary~3.3]{coefficient-mass}: it
is real of degree $D$ with leading coefficient $1$, divisible by the real
polynomial $\prod_i(x-a_i)\prod_i(x+c_i)$, and its coefficients are dominated
by those of $F/f_D$.
\end{proof}

The exclusions cost one root of each sign, and in the worst case the largest
one: $\Pi_k$ omits $a_1,\ldots,a_{k-1}$ and $c_1,\ldots,c_{k-1}$.  This is the
opposite of \cite[Theorem~3.2]{coefficient-mass}, which omits the smallest roots, so
\eqref{eq:bothrowsbound} is the right tool when the moduli are comparable,
and the mass bound it yields has the sharp constant $\frac14$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bothsignssharp}).

\begin{corollary}[Mass at real roots of both signs]\label{cor:bothsignsmass}
Let $F\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero with $|f_D|\ge1$, divisible by
$\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$ with $r_i$ real, repetitions allowed, and $|r_i|\ge R\ge4$.
Then for $1\le k\le\lceil L/2\rceil$,
\[
  b_k(F)\ \ge\ |f_D|\,\frac{(R/2)^{L-2k+2}-1}{2^k-1},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bothmass}
  \Lambda(F)\ \ge\ \Bigl(\Bigl\lceil\frac L2\Bigr\rceil+1\Bigr)\log|f_D|
  +\Bigl\lfloor\frac{(L+1)^2}4\Bigr\rfloor\log\frac R2
  -\frac12\Bigl\lceil\frac L2\Bigr\rceil\Bigl(\Bigl\lceil\frac L2\Bigr\rceil+3\Bigr)\log2 .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $p$ and $n$ count the positive and negative $r_i$; then
$\lceil L/2\rceil\le\max(p,n)$.  Take $\rho=\frac12$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:bothsigns}: every $\rho|r_i|\ge R/2\ge2$, and
$\Pi_k(\tfrac12)\ge(R/2)^{(p-k+1)^++(n-k+1)^+}\ge(R/2)^{L-2k+2}$.  This is the row
bound.  For $k\le\lceil L/2\rceil$ the exponent $L-2k+2$ is at least $1$, so
$X=(R/2)^{L-2k+2}\ge2$, $X-1\ge X/2$, and
$\log b_k(F)\ge\log|f_D|+(L-2k+2)\log(R/2)-(k+1)\log2$.  The leading coefficient
and the $\lceil L/2\rceil$ largest nonleading magnitudes, all nonzero, sit at
distinct positions, so $\Lambda(F)\ge\log^+|f_D|+\sum_k\log^+b_k(F)$ is at
least the sum of these lower bounds; finally
$\sum_{k=1}^{\lceil L/2\rceil}(L-2k+2)=\lfloor(L+1)^2/4\rfloor$ and
$\sum_{k=1}^{\lceil L/2\rceil}(k+1)=\frac12\lceil L/2\rceil(\lceil L/2\rceil+3)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness of the constant $\frac14$]\label{prop:bothsignssharp}
Let $m\ge1$, $R\ge4$, $\lambda\ge1$ and $s_1,\ldots,s_m\in[R,\lambda R]$.  The
polynomial $P=\prod_{j=1}^m(x^2-s_j^2)$ is a monic multiple of the $L=2m$ real
roots $\pm s_j$, all of modulus at least $R$, and
\[
  \Lambda(P)\ \le\ \Bigl\lfloor\frac{(L+1)^2}4\Bigr\rfloor\log(\lambda R)+m^2\log2 .
\]
So the coefficient $\lfloor(L+1)^2/4\rfloor\sim\frac14L^2$ of $\log R$ in
\eqref{eq:bothmass} cannot be increased as $R\to\infty$ with $m$ and
$\lambda$ fixed, where $m^2\log2$ is of lower order, while for roots of one sign
\cite[Corollary~3.4]{coefficient-mass} gives $\frac{L(L+1)}2\log(R-1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The nonleading coefficients of $P$ are $\pm e_j(s_1^2,\ldots,s_m^2)$,
$1\le j\le m$, each between $1$ and $\binom mj(\lambda R)^{2j}$.  So
$\Lambda(P)\le\sum_j\log\binom mj+m(m+1)\log(\lambda R)$, and
$m(m+1)=\lfloor(2m+1)^2/4\rfloor$, $\sum_j\log\binom mj\le m^2\log2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Gaussian-integer roots}\label{sec:gaussian}

For roots in $\mathbb Z[i]$ and integer multiples there is one more piece of
information: an integer polynomial takes Gaussian-integer values at them,
and a nonzero Gaussian integer has modulus at least one.  This charges the
degree of a multiple against its mass (Theorem~\ref{thm:gausscharge}): an
integer multiple splits into blocks, and a block of large degree has large
mass.

Throughout, $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_K\in\mathbb Z[i]$ are distinct, with
$\alpha_j=a_j+ic_j$ and $c_j\ge1$; put $\rho_j=|\alpha_j|$,
$\rho_{\min}=\min_j\rho_j$ and
\[
  P=\prod_{j=1}^K(x-\alpha_j)(x-\bar\alpha_j)
   =\prod_{j=1}^K\bigl(x^2-2a_jx+a_j^2+c_j^2\bigr)\in\mathbb Z[x],
\]
a monic polynomial with $2K$ distinct roots and $P(0)\ne0$.  For
$F=\sum_{i=0}^Df_ix^i$ and $0\le m\le D+1$ write
\[
  F_{<m}=\sum_{i<m}f_ix^i,\qquad G_m=\sum_{i\ge m}f_ix^{i-m},\qquad
  F=F_{<m}+x^mG_m .
\]
We call $m\in\{1,\ldots,D\}$ a \emph{split} of $F$.  A split is \emph{clean
at} $\alpha$ if $G_m(\alpha)=0$, and \emph{$P$-clean} if it is clean at every
$\alpha_j$.  A polynomial $B\in\mathbb Z[x]$ is \emph{$P$-irreducible} if
$B(0)\ne0$ and no split of $B$ is $P$-clean.

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian carries]\label{lem:gausscarry}
Let $F\in\mathbb Z[x]$ have degree $D$, let $\alpha\in\mathbb Z[i]$ with
$F(\alpha)=0$, $\rho=|\alpha|$, and let $1\le m\le D$ (a split of $F$).  Write
$F=F_{<m}+x^mG_m$ with $F_{<m}=\sum_{i<m}f_ix^i$ and
$G_m=\sum_{i\ge m}f_ix^{i-m}$.  Then
$G_m(\alpha)\in\mathbb Z[i]$ and $F_{<m}(\alpha)=-\alpha^mG_m(\alpha)$.  If the
split $m$ is not clean at $\alpha$, that is, if $G_m(\alpha)\ne0$, then $|F_{<m}(\alpha)|\ge\rho^m$, and if
moreover $\rho>2$, some $i<m$ has
\[
  |f_i|\ \ge\ (\rho/2)^{m-i}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$G_m$ has integer coefficients, so $G_m(\alpha)\in\mathbb Z[i]$, and
$0=F(\alpha)=F_{<m}(\alpha)+\alpha^mG_m(\alpha)$.  If $G_m(\alpha)\ne0$ it is a
nonzero Gaussian integer, so $|G_m(\alpha)|\ge1$ and
$|F_{<m}(\alpha)|=\rho^m|G_m(\alpha)|\ge\rho^m$.  If every $i<m$ had
$|f_i|<(\rho/2)^{m-i}$, then
$|F_{<m}(\alpha)|\le\sum_{i<m}|f_i|\rho^i<\rho^m\sum_{i<m}2^{i-m}<\rho^m$, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

The lemma is a Gaussian analogue of the gap principle for lacunary
polynomials~\cite{lenstra-lacunary}, by which a root of small degree of $F$
is a root of both parts across a sufficiently long run of zero coefficients;
here small coefficients below the split play the role of the gap.  Here it is used at every split,
not only at long gaps.

\begin{lemma}[Blocks]\label{lem:gaussblocks}
In the setting of Section~\ref{sec:gaussian}, every nonzero
$F\in\mathbb Z[x]$ divisible by $P$ can be written
\[
  F=\sum_{s=1}^rx^{e_s}B_s,\qquad e_s+\deg B_s<e_{s+1},
\]
with every $B_s\in\mathbb Z[x]$ divisible by $P$ and $P$-irreducible: $B_s(0)\ne0$
and no split $m\in\{1,\ldots,\deg B_s\}$ of $B_s$ has $G_m(\alpha_j)=0$ for every $j$.
Consequently $\Lambda(F)=\sum_s\Lambda(B_s)$, $|\operatorname{lead}B_s|\ge1$,
and $b_k(F)\ge b_k(B_s)$ for every $s$ and $k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $F=x^eF_0$ with $F_0(0)\ne0$; since $P(0)\ne0$, $P\mid F_0$.  Induct
on $\deg F_0$.  If no split of $F_0$ is $P$-clean, $F_0$ itself is the only
block.  Otherwise let $m$ be a $P$-clean split: $G_m(\alpha_j)=0$ for all
$j$, hence $F_{<m}(\alpha_j)=F_0(\alpha_j)-\alpha_j^mG_m(\alpha_j)=0$, and by
reality of the coefficients also $F_{<m}(\bar\alpha_j)=G_m(\bar\alpha_j)=0$.
Since $P$ is monic in $\mathbb Z[x]$ with $2K$ distinct roots, division with
remainder in $\mathbb Z[x]$ shows $P\mid F_{<m}$ and $P\mid G_m$ in
$\mathbb Z[x]$.  Both are nonzero ($F_{<m}(0)=F_0(0)$, and $G_m$ has the leading
coefficient of $F_0$) and of smaller degree than $F_0$; $F_{<m}(0)\ne0$, and
$G_m$ is handled after removing a power of $x$.  Their supports lie in
$[0,m-1]$ and $[m,\deg F_0]$, so the decompositions given by induction
concatenate.  The supports of the $x^{e_s}B_s$ are disjoint and cover that of
$F$, which gives the additivity of $\Lambda$ and the inclusion of the
nonleading magnitudes of each $B_s$ among those of $F$ (the leading
coefficient of $B_s$, $s<r$, is a nonleading coefficient of $F$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Degree charging]\label{thm:gausscharge}
In the setting of Section~\ref{sec:gaussian}, assume $\rho_{\min}>2$, and
let $B\in\mathbb Z[x]$ be $P$-irreducible and
divisible by $P$, of degree $d$.  Then
\[
  \sum_{i<d}\log^+|b_i|\ \ge\ d\log\frac{\rho_{\min}}2,
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad
  \Lambda(B)\ \ge\ \log|b_d|+d\log\frac{\rho_{\min}}2 .
\]
Hence every nonzero $F\in\mathbb Z[x]$ divisible by $P$ has
$\Lambda(F)\ge\sum_s(\deg B_s)\log(\rho_{\min}/2)$ over the blocks $B_s$ of
any decomposition of $F$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussblocks}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every split $m\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$ of $B$ is unclean at some $\alpha_j$, with
$\rho_j\ge\rho_{\min}$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:gausscarry} there is $i<m$ with
$|b_i|\ge(\rho_{\min}/2)^{m-i}$.  For a fixed $i$, the splits charged to it
this way have distinct $m-i\ge1$ with
$(m-i)\log(\rho_{\min}/2)\le\log|b_i|$, so there are at most
$\log^+|b_i|/\log(\rho_{\min}/2)$ of them.  All $d$ splits are charged, which
is the first inequality.  The rest follows from $\log^+|b_d|\ge\log|b_d|$ and
Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussblocks}.
\end{proof}

Compare Proposition~\ref{prop:noangle}, which lies outside this setting: at
most two roots of $x^{2M}+\rho^{2M}$ above the axis are Gaussian integers
(none unless $\rho^{2M}\in\mathbb Z$).  Indeed, if $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ are
two of them, $\alpha'/\alpha$ is a root of unity in $\mathbb Q(i)$, so
$\alpha'\in\{\pm\alpha,\pm i\alpha\}$, and at most two of these four points
lie above the axis.  It
pays $\Lambda=2M\log\rho$ for its degree $2M$, a charge of the form the
theorem gives; what the theorem forbids, at Gaussian roots, is a block of
large degree and small mass.
