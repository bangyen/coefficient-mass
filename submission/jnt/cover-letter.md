Dear Editors of the Journal of Number Theory,

Please consider the manuscript “Displaced-Zero Certificates and the
Coefficient Mass of Polynomial Multiples” for publication in the *Journal of
Number Theory*.

The paper studies how small the coefficients of an arbitrary polynomial
multiple can be when its roots are prescribed. Its main theorem gives a
degree-independent lower bound for every one of the first \(L\) coefficient
order statistics of a multiple with real roots at least 2. Summing these
bounds yields a logarithmic multiplicative-height inequality. For the first
\(L\) primes, the resulting infimum is

\[
\tfrac12 L^2\log L+\tfrac12 L^2\log\log L+O(L^2).
\]

The arithmetic consequences extend the reduced-height line of work on
polynomial multiples. In particular, the paper verifies the
Dubickas--Jankauskas sign hypothesis for positive reciprocal nodes and makes
the inequality \(H(P)\geq |P(1)|\) unconditional for real roots greater than
one, including repetitions. The new mechanism is a tail theorem for
normalized exponential sums with arbitrary prescribed positive-integer
zeros: successively deleting displaced zeros together with the largest
remaining node strictly increases the tail, yielding a sharp upper bound
determined by the initial consecutive run. This removes the degree of the
multiplier from the coefficient bounds.

The manuscript is self-contained. Every theorem, lemma, proposition and
corollary has additionally been formalized in Lean 4, and exact-arithmetic
regression checks accompany the worked examples. These materials are
supporting verification rather than a premise of the mathematical argument.
The stable artifact record is https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22998724.

The manuscript is original, has a single author, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All required disclosures are included in the paper
and separate title page.
